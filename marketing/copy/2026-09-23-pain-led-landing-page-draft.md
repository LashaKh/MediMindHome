# Pain-led landing-page recommendation

Date: 23 September 2026
Status: Proposed copy for review. Not approved, published, or conversion-tested. Existing live copy and archived favorites remain unchanged.

## Why this direction

Lead with the hospital-wide result, name tangible problems underneath, and use a scoped evaluation as the practical next step. The complete library's patient-pathway hook is a useful campaign/discovery entry point; it is too narrow to carry the entire hospital OS vision alone. This recommendation is an editorial synthesis, not a claim that it outperformed the earlier options.

Working pain priorities: lost/delayed payment and opaque costs; repeat staff work; late or inconsistent leadership information; fragmented patient context; slow vendor changes; reporting reconstruction. Switching risk and supplier trust are separate purchase objections. These priorities are hypotheses, not validated Georgian buyer rankings. AI readiness is a strategic opportunity, not established as the primary purchasing motive.

Primary CTA: Request a hospital demo

See the system. Discuss your needs. Decide whether a side-by-side evaluation is right for your hospital.

## 2. Dedicated pain section

### Your people should care for patients. Not hold disconnected software together.

When information stops at each department, the work does not. Your teams fill the gaps—with repeated entries, phone calls, spreadsheets, and billing corrections.

- Staff time goes into repeat work.
- Payment gaps and avoidable costs become harder to see.
- Care teams have to chase the information they need.

Adding another tool does not always fix what happens between them.

## 3. The replacement

### One hospital. One connected way to work.

MediMind brings clinical and business work into one tailored hospital system. Keep patient information connected, make daily work easier to follow, and give leadership a clearer view of how the hospital is performing.

## 4. Continuing development

### Your hospital keeps evolving. Your software should too.

Work directly with the team building your system. Shape improvements around your hospital’s needs, test changes with your staff, and keep making the system better.

## 5. The AI destination

### The future is a hospital that can anticipate. Not just react.

Start with connected information and AI that assists daily work. Our roadmap builds toward a hospital-specific AI system that helps anticipate risks and coordinate clinical and business workflows—with your clinical team retaining control of medical decisions.

## 6. Evaluation

### See the difference. Then decide to switch.

Start with an agreed workflow. Compare a tailored MediMind setup beside your current system. Review the results, staff effort, and transition plan with your team before deciding on a wider rollout.

## Editorial notes — not public copy

- The offer is conditional. “Unlimited development requests” proposes an operating definition for review; it does not silently replace the founder's requested unlimited development hours. Set delivery obligations, priorities, exclusions, pricing term, and advanced AI treatment before publication.
- Price matching refers to agreed software scope, not necessarily unchanged total transition costs or a lifetime price lock. No artificial deadline or limited number of places is created.
- Three-business-day testable changes and the two-week parallel-evaluation offer are omitted from the core copy until readiness, request scope, and timing conditions are agreed. A testable change is not automatically an accepted production release.
- Full hospital replacement remains the ambition. External devices, portals, and services may require integration and a hospital-specific scope assessment.
- Proactive AI is labeled as roadmap. No autonomous clinical operation, inevitable malpractice deadline, or hospital-disappearance prediction is asserted.
- Outcomes are design aims, not guaranteed immediate financial or clinical results. Real proof should distinguish staff time, actual legitimate collections, net cost, and patient outcomes.
- Add verified, permissioned product/customer proof after the solution and before the evaluation. Do not fill that space with an unverified network go-live number or invented testimonial.
- CTA labels are copy proposals, not functioning controls in the report. Before launch verify real demo receipt, lead ownership, confirmation, and error behavior. The complete-library page analysis identified a source-level concern, not a confirmed deployed failure; this task does not repair or deploy the form.
- This document recommends a page narrative, not new standalone routes or additional campaign scope.
