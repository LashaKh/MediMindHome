## First changes to make

1. **Choose one executive outcome.** A working hypothesis is greater operating capacity and clearer financial control without more repeated staff work. Ask friendly hospital heads to rank the benefits before calling it validated buyer language. Keep patient and staff benefit connected to that outcome.
2. **Use precise anchor proof.** Explain what GeoHospitals signed for and what is operating today. Use one approved before/after example if measured; otherwise show the delivered pathway and an appropriately qualified reference. Do not turn 25 contracted hospitals into 25 full-stack live hospitals.
3. **Reduce the main story to about 8-10 slides.** Give each headline one specific message. Put detail in the appendix; the document should work when forwarded without a presenter.
4. **Explain benefits before mechanisms.** One place for the relevant work, less repeated entry, visible progress, fewer handoffs and clearer control. Explain FHIR R4 as an international data standard where relevant; keep architecture detail for IT questions.
5. **Make adoption concrete.** Replace a vague evaluation/phase story with a scoped 90-day success plan, named participants, evidence of acceptance and the hospital's decision points. A plan is not a guarantee that every hospital finishes every department in 90 days.
6. **Keep AI optional and honest.** The existing product must justify itself. Put the broader AI story late or in an appendix, with 2-3 concrete examples only after checking what already works and where. “Your clinicians decide” remains visible for clinical AI.
7. **Resolve the offer text once.** Keep implementation, requests, delivery timing, source-code rights and pricing internally consistent. Do not convert mentor advice into a new legal promise.

## Draft 90-day plan to refine with delivery owners

The day count starts from an agreed kickoff with the necessary people, access and scope ready. These are proposed deliverables and success criteria, not current SLA terms.

### Days 0-30: agree the work and prove the first pathway

| Deliverable | Success criterion |
|---|---|
| Name the executive sponsor, clinical/operations lead and technical contact. | Named owners and one written escalation path. |
| Complete role-based onboarding and relevant system walkthroughs in days 0-7. | Key users can repeat their agreed core tasks; gaps are recorded. |
| Agree the initial departments, permitted data and acceptance measures. | A written scope and baseline with the hospital's own numbers. |
| Inspect the current data and analyzer/interface requirements. | Agreed mappings, access and a bounded migration plan. |
| Prepare the agreed evaluation environment and sample migration. | Counts and critical fields reconciled against the agreed source. |
| Run the first agreed patient/administrative pathway with hospital users. | The pathway completes, and exceptions have named owners. |

### Days 31-60: validate the agreed departments

| Deliverable | Success criterion |
|---|---|
| Configure the agreed departments and staff roles. | Key users demonstrate the department's agreed tasks. |
| Validate selected analyzer, imaging, billing or external connections. | Sample results reach the correct destination with a traceable identity. |
| Compare repeated entry, task time and missing billable services where relevant. | The same measures and cases are used on both sides. |
| Resolve the accepted implementation gaps. | A reviewed exception list distinguishes closed, deferred and blocking items. |
| Run a structured hospital review. | Sponsor and process owners agree what is ready and what needs more work. |

### Days 61-90: make a controlled acceptance decision

| Deliverable | Success criterion |
|---|---|
| Complete the agreed readiness and continuity review. | Named go/no-go authority and an agreed recovery route. |
| Transition only the agreed, accepted scope. | Staff can complete the selected work and use the approved fallback if needed. |
| Complete role training and support handover. | Owners, escalation contacts and support terms are clear. |
| Review live exceptions and changes with the hospital team. | Each material issue has an owner, evidence and an agreed next action. |
| Present the measured outcome and next-scope proposal. | The hospital accepts, extends evaluation or declines against its agreed criteria. |

Use real dates only when the kickoff and delivery capacity are known: “If you decide by [decision date], the agreed [deliverable] can be reviewed by [delivery date].” Do not manufacture a deadline or imply a fixed completion date without a delivery plan.
