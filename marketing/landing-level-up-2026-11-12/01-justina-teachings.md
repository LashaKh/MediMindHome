## 1. Her core framework, in her teaching order

### 1.1 The one mindset change: customer, not company

- "It's all about you, not about we" (D1 00:15:37–00:15:44). Nobody cares what the company is called (D1 00:15:48–00:16:04). She said this is the one change she wants from the day (D1 00:15:19).
- Same rule in the deck review: "stop thinking as a tech people… think about a hospital owner" (1:1 25:01).
- Last line of the 1:1: you must put it "on the words of the user" (1:1 31:00).

### 1.2 Value proposition: what it is and its formula

- A value proposition is **not** a product description. It says why customers pick you instead of the competition (D1 s40; D1 00:53:32–00:53:42).
- It can be one line or three lines (D1 00:06:22–00:06:36).
- **Components, in order (D1 s41; D1 00:53:56–00:54:27):**
  1. Identify all benefits, from the customer's side.
  2. Describe what makes them valuable.
  3. Identify the customer's main problem. She calls this "the most compelling one" for conversion (D1 00:54:03–00:54:15).
  4. Connect the value to that main problem.
  5. Differentiate yourself as the first choice.
- **Two inputs (D1 s56):** customers' experiences + your product's capabilities.
- **Customer VP is not investor VP (D1 s55; D1 01:01:53).** Customers: "Here is what we are going to do for you. Our promise to you." Investors: "Here is who we are and what we do. Company focus."
- **Start with the problem.** "Why don't you just say about the problem?" It closes the gap between you and them (D1 00:54:17–00:54:22).
- **Five seconds, four answers.** People leave websites mainly because they don't understand what you offer, not because it's boring (D1 00:29:16–00:29:20). In the first five seconds they must get: (1) this is for me, (2) the value, (3) how you're different, (4) the next step. Without a next step, "they're gone" (D1 00:29:20–00:30:00; 00:08:28–00:09:12).
- **Never "faster" or "better" without a number.** "Please do not ever use words like faster, better" (D1 00:08:02–00:08:07). "25% better quality, whatever that is" (D1 00:53:50). "Highest quality" means nothing (D1 01:15:10).
- **Plain words.** "It needs to be for your mom and your sister to understand" (D1 01:00:50). She rejects the reply "our market will understand" (D1 00:56:31–00:56:43).
- **Homework:** write **10 completely different** value propositions, not one idea with word swaps (D1 00:12:10; 01:56:02–01:56:11). Test them (D1 01:02:07–01:02:17).

### 1.3 Market sophistication (Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*)

- Sophistication = how many solutions **the buyer** already knows, not how well they know you (D1 00:18:19–00:19:06; D1 s7). "You MUST… market… differently depending on what stage" (D1 s6).

| Level | Situation | What to say | Her example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hello market | First of its kind | Just make the claim | Gillette "Shave yourself" (D1 s9; 00:22:03) |
| 2 What is it | Others copy you | Better/faster/stronger, **with a number** | Toddler ad, also answers "is it safe?" (D1 s11; 00:23:20–00:24:11) |
| 3 How does it work | Crowded; same claims everywhere | A new mechanism: how you keep the old promise | Square blade, lubricant strip (D1 s13; 00:24:18–00:25:10) |
| 4 New mechanism | Everyone explains how | Elaborate the mechanism; tie features to needs | "6 vital improvements" (D1 s15; 00:28:40–00:29:11) |
| 5 Experience | Nobody believes ads | Talk to the person and their identity | Dollar Shave Club, Coca-Cola (D1 s17; 00:30:35–00:35:55) |

- Nobody in the room is at Level 1, because no problem is unsolved today (D1 00:21:26–00:21:56). Most of the batch is at Levels 3–4 (D1 00:21:26; 00:53:42–00:53:45).
- From Level 2 on, handling an objection is part of the message (D1 00:22:53–00:24:11).
- Culture changes meaning: the same product is sold differently by country. She admits her own "European logical brain" misread Asian ads (D1 00:26:43–00:28:22).
- **MediMind's level is not her verdict.** Our pack scored it "probably 3–4" (D1 pack §10). That is our hypothesis.

### 1.4 Jobs to be done (JTBD)

- "People have jobs. Things don't" (D1 s34). People want the hole, then the picture on the wall, then the memory, not the drill (D1 s23; 00:40:00–00:41:16).
- A rival can do the job with no product like yours (3M hooks replace the drill) (D1 s24).
- **Three sides of every job (D1 s35–36):** Functional = what do I want to do? Social = how do I want others to see me while doing it? Emotional = how do I want to feel while doing it?
- Job vs benefit: the job is about the customer; feature and benefit are about the product (D1 s37). The job stays the same for years (iPod, D1 s38).
- JTBD links to retention: "what is the habit am I creating?" (D1 00:43:28–00:43:40).
- Her prompt adds two fields per job: the **trigger moment** that makes it urgent, and **what they did before** (PL1-P4 Step 1).

### 1.5 Problem layers

- Her written method (PL1-P8 Step 2) builds a **problem stack** per customer type:
  - **Surface**: what they can say and would type into Google.
  - **Underlying**: the real cause they may not have named.
  - **Hidden**: the deeper fear, belief or situation behind it.
  - **Future**: what happens if it is not solved in 6–12 months.
- Lead with **one** problem that is validated, urgent, specific enough to be recognised, and different from competitors (PL1-P8 Step 3).
- Write it three ways: rational (data), emotional (the feeling), identity (a threat to who they are) (PL1-P8 Step 4).
- List the problems you must **not** claim to solve (PL1-P8 Step 5).
- Fit statement format: "[Customer] struggles with [problem] because [root cause]. Unlike [alternative], [product] solves this by [mechanism], which means [outcome]" (PL1-P8 Step 6).
- In the lecture: make them "feel the pain as much as possible" so they act now (D1 00:38:42–00:39:02). Pain-aware buyers are a narrow group. To grow, bridge from a wider problem (meditation sold as stress relief) (D1 00:17:52–00:21:11).

### 1.6 BFG = Benefit · Feature · Guarantee

- Think benefit, feature and guarantee when writing the value proposition: "All of this should be included in the value proposition" (D1 01:53:43–01:53:55).
- **Benefit** = why someone should buy (emotion, outcome). **Feature** = what you offer. **Guarantee / social proof** = "assurance to make people trust", e.g. free returns, 90-day guarantee (D1 s70).
- Why BFG: so people know what it does, who it's for, what's in the box, the benefit and the difference from competitors (D1 s69).
- **How to find them (D1 s71):** list every feature → ask why each exists → ask how that "why" connects to the prospect's desires → reach the emotional root.
- Her written version (PL1-P3): 7 questions first (all features; what changes per feature; the single most important outcome; top 3 objections; proof you have; guarantees you can **honestly** offer; why customers chose you). Then a master table **Feature | Benefit | Emotional job | Guarantee or trust signal**, the "source of truth for every piece of marketing copy". Then 3 versions of the strongest combination: rational, emotional, identity.
- Each guarantee needs: what it promises, why it reduces risk, **where in the journey it appears** (PL1-P3 Step 4).

### 1.7 Guarantees and trust in her examples

- Accounting homepage: "Cancel anytime · Safe & secure" sit beside the CTA and answer the fear (D1 00:54:52–00:55:24).
- Trust comes **later** in the funnel: "who is this company, can we trust them, what have they done for others" (D1 00:16:10–00:16:32).
- Trust lines in her page formulas sit **near the CTA**: demo page "above-the-fold trust line" (PL0-P1-S3); free-trial "trust strip" (PL0-P3-S4); privacy line under the form (PL0-P1-S12).
- People argue with opinions, not data. Use a statistic to state the problem, then ask "is this something you experience too?" (1:1 10:36). Her "75 percent of hospitals" was an illustration, not a fact.

### 1.8 UVP tests

- **Five-second test** (1.2 above). Visitors leave when they don't understand (D1 00:29:16).
- **Score each UVP 1–5** on Clarity, Credibility ("given what we can prove") and Desire (link to the emotional job). Keep the top 3 (PL1-P4 Step 4).
- The best one becomes the **hero statement**: "the default headline for the homepage", also the deck opening and first ad line (PL1-P4 Step 6).
- **Test, don't argue.** "My opinion doesn't matter" (D1 00:53:11). To a founder who liked his own hero: your opinion doesn't matter (D1 01:05:12–01:05:17, per D1-DD).
- **A/B rules:** 2 variants, max 3 (D1 00:15:01). About 1,000 visits and 100 conversions per variant ideally (the recording also says 200 once); startups about 500 visits and 50 clicks; never decide on 5 clicks (D1 00:13:36–00:14:34). Tools: Optimizely, Visual Website Optimizer.
- **Separate landing pages for different ICPs**; the source link chooses the page (D1 00:15:06; 01:21:58–01:22:23).
- **Patterns, not one opinion**: no website change from one person's view (D2 S1 00:19:46–00:19:56). Test words with buyers: ≥30 interviews per segment, 100 ideal (D2 S1 00:52:59–00:54:20).
- **Recall test** (1:1 08:12–09:55): after reading, what would they remember? Group the message into about three memorable buckets (her example: too many tools; people's repeated work; the technical side taken care of).
- **Retell test** (1:1 25:01): if I try to repeat what you told me to someone else, can I?
- **No-questions test** (1:1 15:03): "nobody has to ask any questions because it's all self-explanatory."

## 2. Her rules for a landing page and hero (numbered checklist)

### Headline

1. **Lead with the customer's outcome or pain, not the category or the company.** Demo-page headline: outcome-led, under 10 words, names the pain or gain (PL0-P1-S1). Free trial: under 10 words, pain or transformation (PL0-P3-S1). Lead-capture page: 8 words, "problem language not product language" (PL2-P4).
2. **Say who it is for right away.** "Right away… I need to understand that you are talking about me" (D1 00:09:03–00:09:12).
3. **Start with the problem; the system is the vehicle.** "The value is not the system. The system is the vehicle" (D1 01:16:29–01:16:37). Sell point B.
4. **A number beats an adjective.** Quantify, using your own pilot results (D1 01:15:53–01:16:09). No "highest quality" (D1 01:15:10).
5. **Name the consequence, not the mechanism.** Our old hero said programs "don't talk to each other". She heard "it connects something" (D1 01:13:24). (The rule wording is from D1-DD §6; the evidence is her reaction.)
6. **A question headline can carry the CTA.** Canva's "What will you design today?" (D1 00:57:42–00:57:55).
7. **Cause before effect.** "Rank in AI search" leads to "make your website a growth engine"; order them so one is the effect of the other (D1 01:01:28–01:01:45).
8. **Use the buyer's own words.** The sweat brand's line came from interviews ("not in control of my own body") (D1 00:58:53–00:59:10). The message must come from customers, not a trend (D4 01:32:36–01:33:08).

### Pre-headline and sub-headline

9. **Good homepage flow: pre-headline (who it is for) → value proposition → three quantified benefit bullets → call to action** (D1 01:07:01–01:07:26).
10. **A pre-headline can be an industry statistic the buyer recognises**, then your contrast (her demo show-up example) (D1 01:27:35–01:28:29).
11. **Sub-headline = one sentence: who it is for, what changes, how fast** (PL0-P1-S2). Free trial: max 20 words (PL0-P3-S2). Lead-capture and demo pages: 25 words (PL2-P4).

### CTA

12. **Every page needs a next step.** "What is the action you want them to take?" (D1 00:29:44–00:29:51). The homepage goal is lead capture: "the goal is the lead generation" (D1 00:30:21). Without capture you pay for remarketing or rely on hope, "which is not a strategy" (D1 00:30:13–00:30:21).
13. **"Book a demo" alone means nothing.** Say with whom, how long, what I will learn, why I should book and why I should show up (D1 01:47:41–01:47:51; 01:54:16–01:54:29).
14. **CTA button text must include the time commitment.** No generic "Book a demo" (PL0-P1-S4). Not "Register Now / Sign Up" (PL0-P2-S4). Not "Sign Up / Get Started / Try for Free" (PL0-P3-S3). "Get started on what?" (D1 01:01:03).
15. **For MediMind specifically:** "you need to have a call-to-action… What do you want them to do next?" The CTA section's headline should be about the call itself: a 30-minute call, "This is what you're going to learn", and what we will show. "I need to see value from that call myself" (1:1 29:58–30:38).
16. **List what happens on the call: three bullets, each starting with a verb** (PL0-P1-S5). Add who runs it, the length, what they leave with, whether it is recorded, and how fast you reply (PL0-P1 inputs "THE DEMO ITSELF").
17. **Repeat the CTA at the bottom** with one sentence restating the core outcome, same button text (PL0-P1-S13).
18. **Offer a secondary path for people not ready now** (webinar "Can't make it?" CTA with different button text) (PL0-P2-S12). See also §4 not-taken path.

### Where the offer goes ("fifth information")

19. **The offer is not the opening.** When our team wanted to lead with the parallel system, she said she was not ready for that information: "that's a fifth information I need to know" (D1 01:14:38–01:14:50).
20. **Problem first, then offer.** In the deck, opening with the next-five-hospitals offer made her ask "who are you, do I need you?" (1:1 03:00). Add the challenges first so the reader thinks "how did you know?", then the offer (1:1 02:40–05:57).
21. **When you show cost, show your price next to it.** "If you talk about the cost then you have to talk about this… it needs to be together" (1:1 27:09–28:53).
22. **"Compare" must be specific.** Define what is compared: goals or numbers. Otherwise "apples to cherries" (1:1 29:25–29:54).
23. **The offer must sound complete today.** Don't say "optional when ready": it sounds like the product is unfinished, and she would not want to join a company "still doing something" (1:1 19:27; 20:44–20:57). Don't call parts "phases": phases imply nothing works alone. Call them product 1, 2, 3 (1:1 23:55). With phases she concluded Phase 1 alone gives "zero benefits" (1:1 22:41–22:52), which shows the risk.

### Proof

24. **Quantify from your own pilots.** "Quantify that, your pilots… What is the extra revenue they are able to get?" (D1 01:15:53–01:16:09).
25. **Trust line above the fold, near the CTA.** Format: "[number] [customer type] already [outcome]". With no customers yet, a credibility line about the founding team (PL0-P1-S3).
26. **Testimonials: lead with a stat callout; quote → name, title, company** (PL0-P1-S9). Each quote must name a specific result (PL0-P3-S9). If none exist: placeholder, "do not fabricate" (PL0-P2-S10; PL0-P3-S9).
27. **Logo bar caption names an outcome, not "trusted by"** (PL0-P1-S10).
28. **People buy from people.** Use several real voices, e.g. hospital directors, staff and doctors, not one person (D4 00:25:35–00:27:42). (The asker of that question is unidentified; hospitals appear nowhere else in that session, so it was probably meant for MediMind.)
29. **Social proof can lose at one step and win at the next.** Test where it goes (D4 00:53:12–00:53:31; HelloGold tables, slides 41–50).

### Objections

30. **Objection FAQ, four questions:** sales-pressure fear, fit/use-case doubt, timeline/effort, pricing curiosity. 2–3 sentences each (PL0-P1-S11).
31. **Write for the approver too.** A security/trust block written "for the person who has to approve the tool", not the user: data security, uptime, data at the end, one human support signal (PL0-P3-S11).
32. **Objections belong in the message from Level 2 on** (D1 00:22:53–00:24:11). The accounting site's "seriously easy" answers "accounting is hard" (D1 00:55:24–00:55:58).
33. **Scared or defensive buyers:** most people get defensive when told they have a problem. State the problem as data, then ask if they see it too (1:1 10:36). Hospitals fear changing the whole system; about five people decide; still humans (D1 00:37:48–00:38:18).

### Section order (her own templates; pick the one that matches the page's job)

34. **Homepage hero:** pre-headline (who) → value proposition → 3 quantified benefits → CTA (D1 01:07:14–01:07:26).
35. **Book-a-demo page, 13 sections in this order (PL0-P1):** 1 headline · 2 sub-headline · 3 trust line · 4 CTA button · 5 what happens on the call (3 bullets) · 6 pain section (3 statements, customer's exact words, line breaks, no bullets) · 7 how it works (3 steps, 4-word labels, readable in under 10 seconds) · 8 differentiation (max 4 sentences, names the competitor honestly) · 9 testimonials · 10 logo bar caption · 11 objection FAQ (4) · 12 privacy line · 13 bottom repeat CTA.
36. **Campaign story order (D4 slide 52; S1 01:13:48–01:14:10):** establish the problem → show possible solutions → giveaways that build trust → results in advance → present the offer → overcome objections → increase lifetime value.
37. **Story further down the page:** "you tried X, it didn't work, this is why, this is what we'll do for you" (D1 01:41:37–01:41:52).
38. **Deck order she asked for (1:1 04:06–05:57):** who you are for and their challenges → current setup → what changes → the offer → one next step. Don't restate the problem after it is made (1:1 28:58–29:10).

### Length and density

39. **Don't overload.** The first piece exists "to get you a conversation", not to sell everything (1:1 10:36). "You cannot talk about everything at the same time" (D1 01:17:04).
40. **Three problems, not 120.** Ask hospitals their three main problems and focus there; 120 sounds "impossible, also overwhelming" (D1 01:17:04–01:17:24). Niche yourself (D1 01:17:24).
41. **If it takes half an hour to explain, it's not working** (D1 01:36:07–01:36:10).
42. **One angle per message.** "We're not going to come and tell them everything we know" (D4 00:52:58–00:53:08).
43. **Short, scannable blocks.** How-it-works readable in under 10 seconds (PL0-P1-S7). Reading level grade 5–7 (her benchmark list, PL2-P4). She gave **no total page length.**
44. **Words before design.** Redo the text first; "it can be as simple as a… document with the text" (1:1 30:38–30:51).

### What to cut

45. **Filler and buzzwords:** "we help teams", "cutting-edge", "seamless" (PL0-P1 brief); "powerful", "robust", "seamless", "all-in-one", "game-changing" (PL0-P3 brief); "join industry experts", "actionable insights", "don't miss out" (PL0-P2 brief).
46. **Poetic future lines:** "a strong foundation, a path to the future" bring "zero benefits" (1:1 23:55–24:47). "A generation ahead": stay away from buzzwords like "romantic poetry" (1:1 30:12).
47. **Unexplained tech words:** "built for AI", "AI needs separate tools", "personalized clinical record based on best standards" all needed extra questions (1:1 07:12; 12:37–15:53). "Almost for every single one I have to ask an additional question" (1:1 15:03). Say the benefit ("difficult to break", "two times faster") instead (1:1 15:03).
48. **Decorative images.** A dog photo gave "zero value". Show the person with the job done (D1 01:42:31–01:43:16).
49. **The word "founder":** "I've stopped using the word founder. Nobody cares" (D1 01:39:06–01:39:08). (Said about a batch company's hero, not about MediMind.)
50. **"First" claims get challenged:** "is it the very first?" (D1 01:09:10–01:09:21).
51. **Feature lists without meaning:** features alone "mean absolutely nothing" (D1 00:29:01–00:29:11).
52. **Repeated problem slides/sections** (1:1 28:58–29:10).
53. **Words that make you look unfinished** ("optional when ready", "phase") (1:1 19:27–23:55).

### AI wording and scared buyers

54. **Small and medium businesses "are so scared of AI"**; they feel stupid (D1 00:56:58–00:57:15).
55. **People don't understand AI; educate first.** "You think people understand AI. You need to educate them" (D4 01:00:11–01:00:16).
56. **For MediMind:** "Do you think they will understand what is AI?" (1:1 07:59). "Built for AI": non-technical owners won't see the benefit (1:1 15:03). Irakli raised that AI scares operators (1:1 20:28); she did not disagree and moved to the "when ready" wording.
57. **AI jargon heroes fail** ("Supercharge your teams with AI…", "blah blah business talk") (D1 01:00:01–01:01:10).

### Category words

58. **The visitor must know what kind of thing it is**: "Get started on what? Is it an app? Is it a platform?" (D1 01:01:03–01:01:08).
59. **But the category is not the value.** Our hero "One hospital. One operating system" got "I have no idea what this company does" (D1 01:13:24). "Forget about what it is" (D1 01:14:07). "That's what you do. Tell me from the customer perspective" (D1 01:14:22).
60. She herself said "platform" (D1 01:17:19) and "system". She gave **no rule about which category word to use.** Our positioning rule ("operating system") is ours, not hers.

### Scarcity and urgency

61. She **never taught scarcity for MediMind.** In the 1:1 she did not comment on "next five hospitals" as scarcity; she objected that opening with it came before the problem (1:1 03:00).
62. Her prompts treat urgency as an input, not a default: webinar "Is attendance capped / limited seats? YES with number / NO" (PL0-P2 inputs); "urgency and scarcity" is one of nine triggers to choose from (PL2-P2 Part 1).
63. Her urgency tool is **pain**, not deadlines: make them feel the pain "so you need to make a decision about it right now" (D1 00:38:48–00:38:55).
64. Campaigns need a "reason why now" (event-based or evergreen like case studies) (W2D3 S2 00:14:28–00:15:41).

### Forms

65. **Demo form:** required fields + **one** optional qualifying question only (PL0-P1 inputs "FORM FIELDS").
66. **Field-count benchmarks she gives:** 5 or fewer fields converts 120% better; lead-capture page max 2 fields (first name, email); webinar max 3 (PL2-P4).
67. **Calendar embed directly on the demo page**, with a social-proof column beside it (PL2-P4 "BOFU — Demo booking page").
68. **Privacy line under the form:** one sentence saying what you will NOT do with their email; no legal language, no links (PL0-P1-S12; PL0-P3-S15).
69. **Say what happens next** in one sentence (PL2-P4 TOFU and BOFU templates).
