## 1. Storytelling rules (Dina Chaiffetz, 11 Nov)

Dina taught an investor pitch. Most rules carry over to a page, because a page is also read fast by a busy person. One rule does **not** carry over (see 1.6).

### 1.1 Structure
- **A trailer, not the whole movie.** Give a taste so they want to talk to you. About 300 words for 3 minutes. Overdoing it is the most common first-draft mistake. (DINA 05:52, 1:28:38)
  **Web:** the first screen and the first scroll are the trailer. Push detail to secondary pages or the deck.
- **Five parts:** problem, solution, traction, market, team. You pick the order. (DINA 11:05, homework 1:21:21-1:30:31)
  **Web (hospital buyer):** problem → what changes → proof → how we work (the plan) → team → one next step. Market size is an investor fact; leave it off the buyer page.
- **Order of work: script first, then deck, then delivery.** (DINA 11:05; HOR 52:55 "close the script")
  **Web:** write and approve the page copy in plain text before any layout or animation.
- **Classic flow: problem story → build up → product reveal → strong facts → close with "a beautiful vision of the future".** (DINA 1:21:21, 1:24:57-1:28:38)
- **Put the answer right after the story.** Our script had team and competition between the 2 a.m. case and its answer. Move the answer next to the case. (SCRIPTREV "What we are", "Suggested order")

### 1.2 The opening
- **Open with the one thing you most want them to remember.** (DINA 1:24:57-1:28:38)
- **Four allowed openings:** a problem story, an event everyone knows, traction first, personal credibility. (DINA 1:21:21-1:28:38)
  **Web:** for a hospital CEO, "personal credibility + traction" fits best: a practising cardiologist built it, and a 25-hospital network signed. Both are true today (signed, not "live in all 25"; see 3.6).
- **Do not open with "we are an AI ...".** "Everyone's an AI something." Say what the AI does for a critical part of the business. Saying "AI" is becoming like saying "our website". (DINA 41:05, 49:11)
- **No marketing taglines.** Dina cut "we turn frontline hiring around". (DINA 46:45-51:54)

### 1.3 The problem
- **Today's problem, not a future one.** The pain your current product solves. (DINA 11:05-18:21)
- **From the customer's point of view.** For B2B: which business, and who inside it, feels the pain. "I want to feel the pain." (DINA 13:31-16:38)
- **Explain the why.** Say why the problem exists. (DINA 13:31-16:38)
- **Quantify it.** For businesses it is almost always money, time or error rate. "Bonus, but really just do it." (DINA 15:04, 16:38)
- **Bad = vague.** "Employee absenteeism is an expensive problem" fails: how expensive, for whom? Good = concrete scene plus a number. (DINA problem table)
- **Who pays, and so what.** "It's not both." Name the payer and why it hurts them. (DINA class feedback, Asta)
- **Human numbers, not nerdy ones.** A 10-year study is "too nerdy". Say how many people and what changes. (DINA class feedback, Moonlight, 31:06)
- **B2B pain in money.** "What does each empty role cost a hotel?" (DINA class feedback, Joberos)
- **Brianna agrees, for hospitals:** pick the one problem the executive cares about most. "Patients need their doctor" or "doctors need time" is not what executives rank first. Tie it to: more money, more patients seen, less billing overhead. (BRI-22 00:13:04-00:15:34)
- **"Your team is exhausted" is the wrong problem.** "Should I get them a yoga class?" The real problem: mistakes, misalignments, people overstretched, lost reimbursement from insurers and the state. (BRI-27 00:03:41-00:04:53)
- **"Money is lost" means 100 things.** Each problem card must be specific and actionable: payments disputed, staff stretched thin, admin overhead, locked into vendors. (BRI-27 00:05:03-00:09:09)

### 1.4 The case story
- **Our 2 a.m. pancreatitis case is the strongest story we have.** It has a scene, a why (signs buried in a long history) and a cost (20 days in ICU). (SCRIPTREV "Case → Problem")
- **Add one verified number after the case.** "One case shows it can happen. A number shows it happens a lot." Replace abstract lines like "the right tools should not be optional". (SCRIPTREV, from DINA 15:04)
- **Show only the miss first, then the number.** The deck review suggests the "without" path, then a big "1 in 18" ER misdiagnosis figure with a small source (AHRQ, 2022). Keep the MediMind answer for the next section. (DECKREV slide 04)
- **Label it honestly:** "a case from my practice", not "MediMind pilot hospital" (that reads as if MediMind was used). (DECKREV slide 04)
- **The solution must match the problem.** If the problem is a missed sign at 2 a.m., the answer replays that same night: spots the signs, orders the tests, calls the specialists, books the bed, handles billing. (DINA 37:33; SCRIPTREV "What we are")
- **Keep the safety guardrail visible:** "within limits each hospital sets". (DECKREV slide 07; SCRIPTREV)
- **Nicholas: open with the case study, it is "your most important asset".** GeoHospitals first, logo "has to be massive", then a before/after table (time, money, throughput). It creates FOMO and credibility. (NICK 00:16:49, 00:21:53)
- **Brianna: never throw the client under the bus.** For lab errors, frame it as "hospitals generally have this much error; we catch it automatically", not "our client made these mistakes". Tell the client directly that the before/after shows your value. (BRI-27 00:41:35-00:43:41)

### 1.5 What makes people remember
- **One key thing, plus FOMO.** "You are probably not the only one doing this." Find what makes you special (team, access, data, partnership). FOMO is "the magic sauce". (DINA 07:35)
- **Traction framing:** say the big side of the number (98.4% retention, not 1.6% churn). Big names beat small revenue. Describe size when names are unknown ("the biggest insurer in Sweden"). Count what you processed. (DINA traction table, 56:11-1:08:51)
- **One number per sentence.** Three numbers in one line "get mushed together". (DINA 1:04:07)
- **Avoid vanity metrics** (followers, campaigns run). (DINA traction table)
- **Accurate, but framed.** Never lie: people dig in. "We're getting another hospital on board with 600,000 patients" beats "a second clinic said yes". (DINA 1:12:35)
- **Dina's live advice to MediMind (1:09:59-1:12:35):** drop "Georgia's second largest" (too much detail); say "a massive hospital network, 25 hospitals and 1,000 doctors" [confirm]; lead with "1.1 million patient records" [confirm]; frame the next hospital by its size [confirm: patients vs yearly encounters]. (DINA MediMind page)
- **Facts beat polish.** "Even if your storytelling wasn't amazing, but your facts were good, investors will still be very excited." (DINA 1:31:40)
- **Short sentences, no jargon.** Long sentences lose a "five-year-old brain". Explain any problem "like I'm five". (DINA 14:58, 39:24, 46:45-51:54)
- **Claims need numbers.** "Unmatched" or "more accurate than X" means nothing without a figure. (DINA solution table, 51:54)
- **AI only to simplify your own draft.** Otherwise you get "AI slop". (DINA 46:45-51:54) Brianna also flagged copy that "reads like LLM output". (BRI-22 00:28:24)

### 1.6 The one rule that does not transfer
- Dina: **never say "you" to the audience**; investors are not your customer. (DINA 53:05)
  **Web:** on the buyer page the reader **is** the customer, so "your hospital" is right. Keep Dina's rule only for the investor page or pitch.

## 2. Visual design rules (Horacio Decanini, 11 Nov, plus the deck reviews)

**Honest limit:** Horacio gave **no slide count, no word limit and no type sizes**, and no feedback aimed at MediMind. (HOR "Not in this lecture"; DECKREV "Honest limits") Any pixel numbers below are my suggestion, marked as such.

**What looks amateur (from the sources):**
- Too much text, too many icons, too many animations. Irakli: "I want way less"; Brianna agreed. (BRI-27 00:02:39-00:02:58)
- The same words said twice on one slide, once clickable and once not. A list redrawn as a circle that adds no meaning. (BRI-27 00:17:15-00:18:19)
- Interactive extras people must click to understand. "Assume no one's going to click anything." 90% just read what's on the slide. (BRI-27 00:13:27-00:13:41, 00:19:29-00:19:45)
  **Web:** nothing essential inside accordions, tabs, hover cards or videos.
- Titles that don't say anything. Most readers skim headings. (BRI-22 00:40:06, 00:40:55)
- Many faces for a short team line; "the perfect team" claims. (DECKREV slide 05)
- Fake-looking "click to message our client" buttons. (BRI-27 00:16:24-00:16:41)
- Nicholas on our older deck: "very technical, very dry, very numbers, not a lot of visuals". (NICK 00:16:13)

**Skim-order rule (Brianna):** people read the first item and the start of each line. Put the most important item first, and the most important words at the start of the sentence. Her example: not "your whole hospital in one operating system" but **"One operating system for your whole hospital."** (BRI-27 00:15:12-00:15:41)

## 3. Offer, pricing and pilot wording

### 3.1 Value before price
- **Order in every conversation: their pain → the value in their numbers → our price as a share of it.** Don't open with "your current software budget". (PB §15; W5D1 S1 00:34:14-00:38:22)
- **Brianna: don't show the offer before they know what you do.** "Why are you giving me your offer already? I don't even know what you do yet." Order: problem → solution → value → offer. (BRI-27 00:09:51-00:10:16, 00:18:45-00:18:58; BRI-22 00:18:09)
- **Phuong: price from value, not cost or a competitor's bill.** Copying a price is "a perpetual race to the bottom". (PB §15, T4; W5D1 S1 00:20:40-00:22:15)
  **Web:** no public price on the landing page. Explain value; price comes in the conversation after the value equation.
- **"Be transparent with pricing: explain the value ... make it easy to understand."** (W5D1 slide 33) Pricing that needs "a PhD" is a warning sign. (W5D1 S1 01:07:27)
  **Web:** if any pricing words appear, one model a director can repeat in a sentence. Never three stories at once (match, per bed, per seat). (PB §15)
- **Never put a money value on safety outcomes.** (PB §15; W5D1 slides 18-19)
- **"If you're the cheapest ... perceived to be cheap."** Never the word "cheap". (W5D1 digest line 203)

### 3.2 Nothing free without a reason
- **"If you're good at something, never do it for free."** A price on the pilot gives the buyer skin in the game. (PB T1; W5D1 S1 01:08:02-01:09:20)
- **Every free item needs a purpose and a give-get:** decision date, success test, sponsor, reference permission, or pre-agreed paid scope. (PB §15; W5D1 S1 01:13:24-01:13:49)
- **Call it a pilot or evaluation, never a "free trial".** Propose the longest commitment first. (W2D5 S1 02:02:30-02:05:02)
- **"Free for the hospital is not free for the team."** (PB §6; W2D3 §4)
- **Custom things, custom prices.** "This is not a feature. This is a customization." (W3 S1 00:10:29-00:11:18)

### 3.3 Nicholas Ho
- **Stop pricing at parity; charge above market.** "I would straight-up charge even higher than market rate." Raise value to justify price instead of lowering price. Irakli raised Georgian price sensitivity. **Not decided.** (NICK 00:14:59, 00:28:54-00:29:46)
- **Sell the 90-day success plan, not features.** 30 / 60 / 90 days, 5-7 bullets each, with "success criteria", not "focus". "Don't talk about features, talk about what you're going to do for them." (NICK 00:16:49, 00:21:53)
- **Urgency from dated milestones, not countdowns:** "If you decide to work with us by [date], this is what you would expect on [date]." (NICK 00:21:53) BRIEF adds: use real dates only when kickoff and capacity are known; never manufacture a deadline. (BRIEF "Draft 90-day plan")
- **Credibility line:** "our co-founder works in the industry, so it's not created by random software guys". (NICK 00:16:49)
- Nicholas: "How people pitch SaaS, it's like I have tons of features... they don't care." (NICK quotes)

### 3.4 Brianna Salinas
- **No infomercial scarcity.** Drop "limited time offer": "you're not an infomercial". "Next five hospital partners" was acceptable on 22 Oct. (BRI-22 00:15:51-00:16:22)
- **But don't make the slot count confusing.** On 27 Oct the slots were "next two" with one taken. "Just don't make it confusing." A small number next to "25 hospitals" invites the question "you only have one?". (BRI-27 00:11:22-00:12:57)
  **Web:** if we show capacity, say it once, truthfully, without a "taken" counter. BRIEF: "next five" only if the capacity is real. (BRIEF "Scarcity")
- **"Unlimited feature requests" is "so aggressive"**; move any "we build anything" line after the reader knows what we do. (BRI-22 00:16:47-00:18:09) BRIEF: separate the right to request from a promise to deliver every request. (BRIEF "Feature requests")
- **Say "how we work" instead of "try first, then decide":** built alongside current systems, then transition once value is shown. (BRI-22 00:38:44)
- **The parallel evaluation in three plain steps:** "You can keep your system. We run this in parallel. You see the difference." Then talk about switching. Cut repeated words like "tailored", "partnership". (BRI-27 00:21:03-00:21:42)
- **"No vendor lock-in"**, said plainly, replaces "control stays in your hands". Irakli: vendor lock-in is the "most researched or proven pain point". (BRI-22 00:39:40-00:40:06)
- **Keep the funding line** ("we have the fund behind us"): in Georgia it reassures CEOs. (BRI-22 00:28:56-00:29:33)
- **Executives don't care about "one code base".** One or two bullets on what each module does. (BRI-22 00:19:22, 00:20:44)
- **Don't list everything you could do.** Add-ons (patient portal, extra modules) belong in a separate conversation deck, not the first one: "overwhelming", "extremely confusing". (BRI-27 00:21:47-00:26:47)
- **AI future goes late or in a separate deck.** "I don't even know what you do yet, why are you telling me about the future?" (BRI-22 00:25:56, 00:36:08-00:37:07)

### 3.5 Honest proof words
- **"On average... not guaranteed"** instead of guarantees. (W2D5 S1 02:00:12-02:00:23)
- **A "customer" is someone who has paid.** Don't call evaluation hospitals clients. (PB §6; W2D4 S1 00:09)
- **Free evaluations, letters of intent and go-lives are never revenue.** (PB §16; W5D2 S1 00:28:15)
- **Reference logos only work if the reader recognises them.** Peer framing: "We helped [peer they know] do X." (W2D2 S1 01:45:59-01:47:45) GeoHospitals is recognisable in Georgia: use it big (NICK).
- **The source-code line must stay exact.** "You own the first version" vs "if MediMind doesn't deliver" are two different promises. Don't silently swap. Lawyer first. (BRIEF "Source code"; PB T17; AI "Before the first send")

### 3.6 Facts the page must not stretch
- **25 hospitals = signed, not "live across all 25".** (DECKREV slide 01, 08; BRIEF item 2)
- **15 months, not 13** (the script's number). (DECKREV fact table)
- **Spoken numbers to confirm before use:** 1,000 doctors; 1.1M patient records (moved or being moved?); 600,000 patients (per year? encounters?); "~2M patient encounters". (DINA MediMind page; SCRIPTREV Validation)
- **Brianna's Lasha figures ("2,000+ lab requests, zero-point-something issues") are unverified.** (BRI-22 summary)
- **Benefits are not yet validated by hospital heads.** Irakli: "many of these are not really validated". Ask friendly executives to rank them. (BRI-22 00:34:00-00:35:05)

## 4. Sales, demo and funnel rules that touch the page

### 4.1 The CTA
- **One primary call to action.** (PB §5)
- **A useful offer beats "let's meet for 30 minutes".** Brianna: "Let's meet 30 minutes feels very AI, doesn't feel like a real person." Offer a **free evaluation plan**, plus a comparison with what other hospitals do better. Remove "choose a time in the team calendar". (BRI-27 00:27:01-00:28:12)
- **For large buyers, "book a call / consultation" beats "book a demo".** Demoing in call 1 risks "seen enough" and drop-off. (W2D2 S3 00:17:43-00:24:22)
- **Specific time beats a Calendly link** in outreach ("Tuesday or Wednesday morning?"). (W2D2 S1 01:20:09-01:20:52)
  **Web:** a form that asks for preferred day is warmer than a raw calendar embed.
- **Nicholas: close without a sales demo.** Show the system in onboarding (days 0-7 of the 90-day plan). He admits this is untested here. (NICK 00:25:46-00:27:41) BRIEF: no universal demo ban; keep a short proof option. (BRIEF "Demo placement")
  **Web:** no "watch the full product tour" as the main CTA. A short proof clip can sit lower.
- **Don't cut off conversations with too much information.** "Your goal is just to make it interesting enough that they want to talk to you." (BRI-27 00:28:29-00:28:46; also 00:19:51-00:20:07)
- **Reference calls are a trade, not a button.** "Happy to have you talk to our partner. We'd love to talk to your CFO or billing in exchange." (BRI-27 00:16:24-00:16:41)

### 4.2 Qualification and next step
- **The page is MQL, not SQL.** Signals that count: requested a call, asked for integration or security info, referred by a customer or investor. (W2D2 D1 s6, s8)
  **Web:** a short form (name, hospital, role, top problem). Don't ask budget or authority online: "Nobody answers budget or authority questions in writing." (PB §4; W2D2 S3 00:08)
- **A dated next step after every contact; book within 72 hours.** (PB §5; W2D3 S1 01:50; W1D3 S1 00:57:59)
  **Web:** the confirmation message promises a reply time and proposes a slot.
- **Different decks for different problems is "great, fantastic"; all problems in one is "not great".** (BRI-27 00:07:20-00:07:40)
  **Web:** one main page for the CEO, with problem-specific pages (billing disputes, lab errors, vendor lock-in) for follow-up links.
- **CEO = cost / risk / visibility; clinicians = less re-entry, less hassle.** Separate buyer and user value; a page per use case. (W2D1 rule 6, S3 00:13:33-00:17:02)
- **Track lead source from day one** (warm intro, outreach, inbound). Data not recorded can't be added later. (PB §16; W5D2 S1 00:13:23-00:14:49)
  **Web:** a "how did you hear about us" field or hidden source tag on the form.
- **Recipients want proof you're real:** the best email is untrustworthy without a website to check. (W2D2 S1 01:10:16-01:10:45)
  **Web:** the page's job is often to make a warm intro credible. Real names, real photos, real hospital.
- **Value props go in every channel**, not just the site. (W2D1 S3 00:03:37-00:04:24)

### 4.3 Product-led growth ideas that fit a hospital sale (Giacomo, W3)
- **Strong core usage first, growth loops second.** (W3 S1 01:12:05-01:12:46)
- **Permissioned hospital-reference loop:** good result → intro to the next decision-maker. (PB §9)
- **Company-generated content:** an anonymised report on Georgian hospital operations (Carta-style). Never patient data. (W3 S1 00:58:03-01:05:17; PB §9)
  **Web:** a gated benchmark or report is a fit lead magnet for CEOs.
- **Request pages (Zapier style):** a page per requested capability, collect interest, prioritise by demand. (W3 S1 01:03:00-01:03:31)
- **Case studies are part of the product:** result → approved story → next sale. (W2D4 S1 01:33:02-01:36:21)

## 6. Checklist for page builders

- [ ] Copy written and approved as plain text before any layout (DINA 11:05; HOR 52:55).
- [ ] 2-3 attribute words written at the top of the design brief (HOR 13:15).
- [ ] Hero passes the skim test: important words at the start of each line (BRI-27 00:15:35).
- [ ] Hero does not open with "AI" or a tagline (DINA 41:05, 46:45).
- [ ] The problem section names one executive problem in money, time or errors, with one verified number (DINA 15:04; BRI-22 00:13:04).
- [ ] No problem card says something vague like "money is lost" or "team exhausted" (BRI-27 00:03:41-00:05:47).
- [ ] The 2 a.m. case comes first, then a sourced number, then the matching "with MediMind" replay right after (SCRIPTREV; DECKREV slide 04).
- [ ] "Within limits each hospital sets" is visible wherever AI makes decisions (DECKREV slide 07).
- [ ] GeoHospitals proof is big, exact and says "signed" (not "live in all 25") (NICK 00:16:49; DECKREV slide 01).
- [ ] Every number is confirmed by Irakli and appears once, one per sentence (DINA 1:04:07, 1:12:35).
- [ ] Every number on the page is one the team actually says (HOR 27:54; DECKREV rule 13).
- [ ] Offer appears only after problem, solution and value (BRI-27 00:18:45).
- [ ] No public price, no "price match", no "unlimited features", no "limited time offer" (W5D1; BRI-22 00:15:51, 00:16:47).
- [ ] Evaluation described as "keep your system, we run in parallel, you see the difference"; never "free trial" (BRI-27 00:21; W2D5 02:02).
- [ ] A 90-day plan section with success criteria, not a feature list (NICK 00:21:53; BRIEF).
- [ ] "No vendor lock-in" said plainly; source-code wording is the lawyer-approved version only (BRI-22 00:40:06; PB T17).
- [ ] Team: two founders, one concrete proof each; no "brilliant" or "perfect team" (DECKREV slide 05; BRI-22 00:29:33).
- [ ] AI future and add-on modules are low on the page or on separate pages (BRI-22 00:25:56; BRI-27 00:23:53).
- [ ] Nothing essential hidden in accordions, tabs, hover or video (BRI-27 00:13:33, 00:19:29).
- [ ] One primary CTA offering something useful (a free evaluation plan), not "meet for 30 minutes" (BRI-27 00:27:01).
- [ ] Form is short, asks the top problem, tags lead source, and promises a reply time (W5D2; PB §5).
- [ ] One font family, loaded; one icon family; one photo style; grid holds at phone width (HOR 31:25-49:13; DECKREV system check).
- [ ] Sections alternate dense and airy; 1-2 brand-colour punch sections (HOR 26:13, 33:18).
- [ ] Each data block has one highlighted number someone can name in 3 seconds (HOR 29:36).
- [ ] Real, permissioned photos and voices only; language checked against `positioning/` ("operating system", "one of the first") (PB T16, T8).
