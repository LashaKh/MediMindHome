# Book a Demo Page

> **Status and completed inputs.** This is proposed page copy for a founder-led 25-minute pathway demo, not a description of the current `/request-demo` experience. The current source calls it 30 minutes, requires nine fields over three steps, and its submit handler logs data locally before displaying a receipt message; a real delivery and scheduling path needs verification before this page launches. The privacy sentence in Section 12 is proposed policy copy requiring adoption. Pain lines are buyer-language hypotheses, not customer quotes.
>
> **Product:** MediMind. One-line description: a hospital operating system that brings clinical and business work into a shared patient and operational record. Core mechanic: follow a patient pathway across roles → complete and review work in connected workflows → inspect where information, time, or legitimate charges need attention. Differentiator: founder-led local adaptation plus an agreed, measured parallel evaluation of the hospital's own priority workflow. The FHIR R4 foundation is a technical design choice, not a promised result.
>
> **Buyer:** CEO, owner, or group operator of a private hospital network or multi-profile hospital in Georgia, with clinical, operations, finance, and IT counterparts. Proposed pain 1: “Why does one patient make so many people enter the same thing?” Pain 2: “I get three versions of the same number.” Pain 3: “Every useful change becomes another request to another vendor.” Likely workarounds to test: spreadsheets, calls, manual reconciliation, current supplier changes, and point tools. Booking fear: an unqualified sales pitch or a risky rip-and-replace proposal. Proposed 90-day success: one agreed pathway measured against its baseline for repeat entries, handoff time, or another hospital-selected process measure; no outcome is promised.
>
> **Alternatives:** The hospital's current supplier, a focused integration or automation layer, and a broad replacement platform are the relevant choices; named vendors depend on the account. Their gap is **conditional**: a local evaluation may show that a needed cross-department change is still hard, costly, or slow. MediMind's honest position is that the buyer can compare an agreed workflow and transition effort before deciding whether a broader change is justified.
>
> **Proof:** Customer/user count, result statistic, attributed testimonials, review score, and logo permissions: not verified for public use. Founder credential: Lasha Khoshtaria is MediMind's physician CEO; the team has founder-led hospital implementation experience, with current scope and outcomes to be substantiated separately. GeoHospitals' signed agreement is founder-reported, not an authorized logo, deployment-count, or outcome claim.
>
> **Demo:** Proposed duration 25 minutes. Host: Lasha Khoshtaria, physician CEO, subject to his calendar. Show: a synthetic patient's shared pathway, the handoff from clinical work to operational/financial follow-up, and a sample baseline-to-evaluation scorecard. Leave with: one candidate pathway, proposed measure, required hospital owners, and next-step questions. Recording: no recording promise; ask before recording any call. Response time: no stated service promise. **Recommended form:** first name, work email, hospital/group name; one optional question, “Which patient pathway creates the most repeated work?” Current live source instead requires first and last name, email, company name and size, industry, job title, facility type, and provider count.

## Section 7 — How it works

**1. Choose one pathway.** Name a recurring patient journey where clinical, operational, and business work cross.

**2. Follow the work.** See how MediMind presents the same case and its handoffs to the people responsible for each step.

**3. Agree on a measure.** If the fit looks promising, define a baseline, scope, and owners for a readiness-dependent parallel evaluation.

## Section 8 — Differentiation section

Your current supplier may already support much of your hospital, and a focused tool may fix a single bottleneck. MediMind is worth comparing when the problem follows the patient across departments and separate fixes leave people reconciling the same work. In the demo, we follow one pathway and name what the product can show, what would need adaptation, and what should be measured. A production change happens only after your hospital's review and sign-off.

## Section 11 — Objection FAQ

**Will this turn into a sales pitch?** We use the 25 minutes to trace one pathway and hear where your hospital's work differs. You can end with a measurement question and no commitment to an evaluation.

**Will this fit our workflows?** We will mark which steps we can demonstrate now and which need configuration, integration, or development. Bring one clinical or operational colleague if their view would help test the fit.

**How much work would an evaluation take?** The demo needs only a pathway you can describe; an evaluation needs agreed scope, owners, access and readiness checks. A tailored parallel evaluation is a proposed two-week path when those prerequisites are met, with production sign-off separate.

**What would it cost?** For eligible early partners, the founder's proposed offer is to match current software pricing within agreed terms and include continuing development within an agreed scope. We will discuss implementation, integrations, support, and any advanced AI costs explicitly before a proposal; no total-cost or permanent price-lock promise is implied.
