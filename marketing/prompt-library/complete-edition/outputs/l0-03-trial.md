# Free Trial Page

> **Status and completed inputs.** This is proposed copy for a guided synthetic-data sandbox, not a currently verified self-service product or an approved commercial policy. The recommended offer is **14 days, no credit card, no automatic charge**, with an option to stop at any time; the team must implement and approve those terms before publication. This is separate from a readiness-dependent, two-week parallel evaluation inside a hospital and from live clinical deployment. Trial-end access, retention/deletion rules, support capacity, pricing, security controls, and any uptime commitment need operational decisions. The privacy sentence in Section 15 is proposed policy copy requiring adoption. Pain lines are buyer-language hypotheses, not customer quotes.
>
> **Product:** MediMind, a hospital operating system connecting clinical and business work around a shared patient and operational record. First-session “aha”: use a supplied synthetic patient to follow one pathway across roles and mark where repeated entry or a missing handoff could be measured. Recommended time to this first result: the first guided session, assuming the sandbox is provisioned and the workflow is validated; no minute-level setup promise. Three core activities: trace a synthetic patient case, inspect the handoff between teams, and mark one evaluation question.
>
> **Trial structure:** Opt-in guided sandbox, 14 calendar days, no card, synthetic cases only; this is a recommendation. Included scope: a reviewed sample pathway, role views needed to follow it, a simple pathway worksheet, and one guided orientation if capacity is approved. Excluded from the sandbox: real patient data, production integrations, hospital-specific configuration, clinical use, and a guaranteed production-ready feature set. At trial end: **[PLACEHOLDER: approved pause, export, retention, and deletion policy]**; no paid conversion without a separate proposal and explicit agreement is the recommended billing rule. Cancel/stop anytime: recommended. Free plan after trial: undecided; do not advertise one.
>
> **Customer:** Georgian private hospital CEO/owner or an assigned clinical/operations lead and IT counterpart evaluating a cross-department workflow. Company type: private network or multi-profile hospital, with fit assessed by workflow complexity and evaluation readiness rather than a fixed bed count. Proposed pain: “I cannot ask people to change systems until I see what the patient journey looks like.” Workaround: screenshots, slides, vendor demos, spreadsheets, and manual walkthroughs. First fear: hidden billing or effort. Second fear: synthetic success may be mistaken for safe real-world readiness. Proposed day-seven milestone: document one pathway, name its owner, and choose one baseline measure; this is a user task, not a predicted product outcome.
>
> **Proof:** Verified user/company count, customer result, attributable testimonials, third-party review score, and authorized logos: unavailable. Founder-led implementation experience can be described, but it is not a substitute for independent outcome data. Security certifications and uptime/service-level numbers: none verified for this page. Proposed data handling rule: use only supplied synthetic cases and do not enter patient information; any storage, access, deletion, and incident promises need technical and policy approval.
>
> **Sign-up and price:** Proposed required fields: work email, name, and hospital/group name; optional question after email: “Which handoff do you want to explore?” SSO/social login: none verified, so do not advertise. Public post-trial starting price: unavailable. Proposed path after sandbox: discuss a scoped hospital evaluation and a separate commercial proposal. Founder-defined early-partner intent includes matching current software price and continuing development within agreed terms; it does not establish a fixed public plan, total implementation price, advanced AI costs, or a free plan.
