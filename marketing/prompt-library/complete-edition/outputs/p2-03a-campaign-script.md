# Part 2, Prompt 3A — Build Your Campaign Concept & Video Script

> **Status and required inputs (23 September 2026).** The first buyer is the synthetic accountable Georgian private-hospital operator from Prompt 9. Prompt 8's working surface problem is repeat work along one patient pathway; the hidden problem is fear that a replacement will make care and operations worse. The strongest working UVP is to **find and test** that repeat work, at market-sophistication Level 4 (“New Mechanism”). Prompt 2's top angle is a low-risk, useful first action: map the hospital's own pathway, then compare a tailored version beside current work before switching. The campaign must drive a **held, qualified founder-led pathway demo**, initiated through a verified request route or direct founder reply. Production reality is **one founder, a phone camera, paper cards or a simple synthetic screen recording, natural light, and no paid production budget**. The most painful moment to dramatize is staff having to ask or re-enter what another department already knows while a patient waits for the next step. There is no traceable buyer quote explaining why a customer chose MediMind; no line below is attributed to a customer. The founder reports a signed GeoHospitals agreement; no customer outcome or live-site count is implied.

## Part 1 — Campaign concept

**Premise:** This campaign is for a private-hospital leader who sees one patient create repeated work across departments, and it shows them that a safer first step is to map and compare one pathway before considering a wider software change.

**Pattern interrupt — the question they may already be asking.** Open the main asset with the founder holding three simple cards labeled “order,” “result,” and “bill,” then moving the same blank patient card between them. Exact first five seconds: **“Why does one patient create so much extra work?”** The question is original proposed copy, not a buyer quotation. The cards make the problem visible without using patient information or pretending a fabricated case is real. The two A/B variations deliberately test different openings against this main question-led version.

**Campaign CTA (five words):** **“Map my hospital's first improvement.”** The promised outcome is a concrete pathway map and next measurement question, not an instant hospital-wide transformation. The first conversation is recommended as 25 minutes, led by the founder. A tailored parallel evaluation in two weeks is the founder's readiness-dependent offer after scope and approval, not what every viewer automatically receives.

**Three-act path:** Problem—one patient story gets recreated across roles. Mechanism—the team follows one shared path from task to task on a FHIR R4 foundation and can compare it beside current work. Relief—the leader can inspect what changed and decide with clinical and IT colleagues whether a broader move is justified. AI may assist with defined tasks for human review, but the story does not need an autonomous clinical claim to be compelling.

## Part 2 — Video script: founder on camera, phone format

**Target running time: 78–85 seconds at an ordinary speaking pace.** Film vertically with a clean desk and daylight; crop a horizontal version from a wider phone frame if needed. The founder may speak as a physician and CEO, but should sound like a colleague mapping work, not a narrator advertising a miracle. Cards and screens contain invented names or no names at all. Every onscreen product capture must come from a synthetic/demo environment, with no actual patient, staff, payer, or contract data.

| Time / act | Exact founder words | What the camera shows | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–5s / opening** | **“Why does one patient create so much extra work?”** | Close view of one blank patient card passing from a paper “order” card to “result” to “bill.” Founder looks up at camera on “work.” | “ONE PATIENT. TOO MUCH REPEAT WORK.” |
| **5–17s / problem** | “A doctor orders a test. Someone calls to check whether it happened. Someone else finds the result. Finance still has to work out what to bill.” | Founder places a small question mark between each card. No product visible. | “Order → chase → result → reconcile” |
| **17–27s / hidden problem** | “Each person is doing their job. But the journey between them can become another job for your staff.” | Stack blank paper slips under the same patient card. Pause so the image lands. | “The handoff becomes work.” |
| **27–41s / mechanism** | “So we start with one real pathway at your hospital. We map who needs to act, what information they need, and where the same work is repeated.” | Whiteboard/paper diagram with three role labels and arrows; founder circles one repeated step. If a screenshot is used, switch only to synthetic demo data. | “Map one pathway.” |
| **41–57s / mechanism** | “MediMind brings the clinical and business steps into one hospital operating system. Your team can compare that path beside the way it works today.” | Show two paper lanes labeled “today” and “proposed”; reveal one shared task and patient context on a synthetic screen if available. Do not show a flashy dashboard unrelated to the example. | “Compare beside today's work.” |
| **57–69s / relief** | “Then we measure the handoff, the staff effort, and the exceptions. Your clinicians and IT team help decide what is ready—and whether a wider change makes sense.” | Founder places a simple three-column card: “work,” “exceptions,” “decision.” A handwritten checkbox remains empty. | “Measure. Review. Decide.” |
| **69–72s / transition** | “Bring me one handoff your hospital keeps repeating.” | Direct to camera; keep the circled pathway visible. | “ONE HANDOFF” |
| **72–82s / closing ten seconds** | **“In 25 minutes, we'll map the first improvement worth testing. Map my hospital's first improvement.”** | Hold a clean CTA card with the verified public route or a direct founder contact path. | “MAP MY HOSPITAL'S FIRST IMPROVEMENT” |

The spoken script is about 155 words, leaving time for visual pauses. A fast read would undercut the tone; if the founder reaches the CTA too early, let the two-lane comparison remain on screen rather than adding claims. If the full cut runs past 90 seconds, cut the line about billing in the first act before accelerating speech.

**Director's three instructions:**

1. Keep the opening quiet and slightly puzzled; speak directly to one hospital operator, not to a crowd. Let the paper-card movement supply the interruption.
2. Make each cut coincide with one handoff. Use large, readable labels and high contrast; mobile viewers should understand the visual with sound off and captions on.
3. End on the founder's face and one CTA. Do not place an unverified customer logo, “25 hospitals live,” a performance percentage, or an artificial deadline on screen.

**15-second organic cut-down (same question-led campaign):**

| Time | Exact words | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | “Why does one patient create so much extra work?” | Same three cards and blank patient card. |
| 4–9s | “Pick one handoff. We'll map what repeats.” | Circle one repeated arrow on paper. |
| 9–15s | “Compare beside today's work. Map my hospital's first improvement.” | Two lanes, then CTA card. |

The cut-down invites the same action and does not introduce an unsupported result or a second destination.

## Part 3 — Two shorter A/B script variations

These are **ready-to-film creative tests**, not instructions to buy ads now. They can be posted organically first; paid LinkedIn/YouTube distribution is conditional on a real budget, a functioning lead route, and evidence that the message attracts qualified hospital operators. Each follows problem → mechanism → relief, but changes the opening so the team can learn which explanation earns a useful reply.

### Variation A — visual contrast interrupt, 22–25 seconds

**Audience:** cold relevant hospital leaders; **trigger:** commitment and consistency—the leader can check one visible example without endorsing a replacement. The contrast appears in the opening split screen.

| Time | Exact script | Visual / act |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | “Same patient. Two very different ways to handle the handoff.” | Left: three separate blank cards and arrows; right: one connected path. No MediMind logo yet. **Problem.** |
| 5–12s | “Here, staff ask and copy. Here, the next task stays with the patient context.” | Founder points to each lane; label the right lane “proposed,” not “proven result.” **Mechanism.** |
| 12–23s | “Which path works better for your hospital? Map one handoff, compare both, then decide. Map my hospital's first improvement.” | Founder circles a single handoff, then CTA card. **Relief and CTA.** |

This variant tests whether seeing a concrete alternative before hearing the question improves comprehension. It does not show actual patient data or assert that MediMind has already reduced a real customer's work.

### Variation B — honest social-proof interrupt, 25–29 seconds

**Audience:** warm retargeting or existing leads; **trigger:** social proof that lowers the perceived risk of engaging, immediately bounded by an invitation to verify the buyer's own case. Social proof is a founder-reported procurement fact, **not** a customer testimonial or measured outcome. Before public release, confirm the approved wording about the signed network agreement and avoid using the customer's name or logo without permission.

| Time | Exact script | Visual / act |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | “A Georgian hospital network signed with MediMind.” | Founder on camera; text “Signed network agreement — founder-reported.” No logo, facility count, or invented result. **Social-proof opening.** |
| 5–15s | “That is a reason to look, not proof for your team. Pick one handoff your staff repeats.” | Founder lays out the same blank “order → result → bill” cards. **Problem.** |
| 15–28s | “We'll map a connected path, set a measure, and let your clinical and IT team decide. Map my hospital's first improvement.” | Two-lane paper diagram and CTA card. **Mechanism and relief.** |

If the agreement cannot be referenced publicly with approved wording, **hold this variation**. Replace the social-proof opening only with a source-checked, customer-approved implementation fact; never substitute a fabricated quote, outcome, or “live at 25 hospitals” badge. The main and Variation A remain filmable without this variant.

### Production and destination checklist

Keep this as a short execution checklist, not a new approval workflow: phone at eye level, natural light, quiet room, paper labels in large type, clean synthetic screen capture if used, accurate captions, one CTA, and no patient information. The proposed destination is the existing `/request-demo` route, which currently says 30 minutes in source; align that page with the recommended 25-minute offer if the strategy is adopted. Source inspection found a simulated form submission, so verify deployed behavior and implement actual receipt before linking a public campaign to it. Track accepted request, qualified booking, and held demo as separate events. The video itself does not claim a scheduled webinar, self-serve clinical trial, response-time SLA, or clinical outcome.
