## Part 1 — Ad copy

The three LinkedIn assets address different awareness states. Each body is under 150 words, has one emotion, and uses the exact campaign CTA. Paid audience definitions and retargeting require consent, working measurement, and an approved budget; the same copy can be tested in founder posts or permissioned introductions first.

### LinkedIn sponsored content — three versions

**A. Cold / TOFU — curiosity.**

- **Hook, first two lines:**

  Why does one patient create so much extra work?  
  Follow the handoff, not just the chart.
- **Body:** “An order becomes a result. The result needs a decision. The decision reaches billing. If staff have to ask, copy, or reconcile at every step, that journey creates another job alongside care. We draw one patient pathway with your team, show where work repeats, and agree what would be worth measuring before anyone discusses a wider system change. Start with the pathway you already know is difficult.”
- **Primary copy CTA:** **Map my hospital's first improvement** → verified `/request-demo` route. **Native sponsored button:** use a platform-supported preset such as “Request demo” if available; verify the actual options in the account.
- **Visual:** Founder moves a blank patient card across three paper cards: order, result, bill. No patient data.
- **Psychological trigger:** Recognition of a familiar task, followed by a small, inspectable first step.

**B. Warm / MOFU — cautious hope.**

- **Hook, first two lines:**

  Why does one patient create so much extra work?  
  What would a fair comparison look like?
- **Body:** “A focused tool may fix one step. Your current supplier may be able to change another. If the same handoff keeps crossing departments, compare the whole pathway. In a 25-minute founder-led walkthrough, we map the current work, show a connected proposed path using synthetic data, and name the staff and IT conditions for any parallel evaluation. You keep the decision. The first result is a clear question to test, not a promise that the hospital changes overnight.”
- **Primary copy CTA:** **Map my hospital's first improvement** → `/request-demo`. **Native sponsored button:** supported “Request demo” preset, if available.
- **Visual:** Two paper lanes labeled “today” and “proposed,” with one repeated arrow circled.
- **Psychological trigger:** Control over change and transparency about the test.

**C. BOFU visitor who has not requested a demo — confidence.**

- **Hook, first two lines:**

  One patient. Too much repeat work.  
  Bring us the handoff that matters most.
- **Body:** “You may already know which workflow makes your team call another department, re-enter a result, or reconcile a bill. Bring that example to a 25-minute pathway demo. We will leave with a map of the current steps, one candidate improvement, and the first measure your clinical, operations, and IT colleagues could review. If the current supplier can solve it with less disruption, that belongs in the comparison too.”
- **Primary copy CTA:** **Map my hospital's first improvement** → `/request-demo`. **Native sponsored button:** supported “Request demo” preset, if available.
- **Visual:** Founder holds the three-column card from the video: “work / exceptions / decision.”
- **Psychological trigger:** A defensible next decision; no implied tracking of an individual's visit in the copy.

## Part 2 — Four-email campaign sequence

These are complete **unsent** drafts for an opted-in or otherwise permissioned audience. Source-list size, email service, and opened/clicked events are unverified. A message should only be sent when its segment and delivery are real. Subject lines are eight words or fewer; preview text is 40 characters or fewer. The product is absent from Email 2. The third email uses a *competitor customer's public review* and labels it as such; it is not MediMind social proof.

### Email 1 — Campaign launch, day 1

**Subject:** Why does one patient create extra work?  
**Preview:** Trace one handoff with us.  
**Opening line:** Why does one patient create so much extra work?

**Full body:**

Hello [First name],

A patient can move from an order to a result to a bill while the information is still carried by calls, copied notes, and checks between teams. Each person may be doing the right thing. The handoff itself can become extra work.

We are starting with one practical question: which patient pathway does your team repeat most often? In a 25-minute founder-led walkthrough, we can draw that pathway, show a proposed connected version with synthetic data, and name one measure worth checking with your team. It is a first look, not a request to switch systems.

**CTA link text:** [Map my hospital's first improvement](/request-demo)

### Email 2 — Value delivery, day 3, opened but did not click

**Subject:** Map the handoff before changing software  
**Preview:** A five-minute pathway check.  
**Opening line:** Here is a simple way to see where the work repeats.

**Full body:**

Hello [First name],

Take one recent patient journey and draw four boxes: the first request, the result, the next decision, and the financial close. Under each box, write who owns it and what information they need. Put a circle around every place someone calls, copies, waits, or checks an earlier step.

Then ask two questions: Which circle happens repeatedly? Which one has a cost or safety consequence you can actually observe? The answer may point to training, a supplier change, a narrow tool, or a wider workflow issue.

You can use the blank worksheet without sharing patient details.

**Soft CTA link text:** [Use the pathway worksheet](/pathway-assessment) — proposed P01 route; replace the link only after it exists and works.

### Email 3 — Process proof, day 6, opened but not converted

**Subject:** What a real reviewer valued  
**Preview:** Breadth matters. So does fit.  
**Opening line:** A hospital-software review gave us a useful counterexample.

**Full body:**

Hello [First name],

An ophthalmic technician described moving **to Epic** because the previous system did not provide the breadth and customization her team wanted. In her August 2026 [public review](https://www.capterra.com/p/122278/EpicCare-EMR/), she reported easier access to notes across providers and useful tailoring after the switch. That is a competitor's success story, not a MediMind result.

It is also a good test for any new system: does it make the *whole* job easier for your staff, without taking away the parts they already value? Bring one pathway to our founder-led walkthrough. We will map the current work and the evidence needed for a fair comparison.

**CTA link text:** [Map my hospital's first improvement](/request-demo)

### Email 4 — Truthful urgency, day 10; under 100 words

**Subject:** The handoff will repeat next month  
**Preview:** Start with one measurable path.  
**Opening line:** If the same pathway keeps creating extra work, waiting has a cost worth measuring.

**Full body:**

Hello [First name],

If staff repeat the same handoff next month, the calls and reconciliation will still need an owner. We cannot tell you the size of that cost without looking at your workflow. We can spend 25 minutes mapping one path and agreeing what your team should measure before any wider change.

**CTA link text:** [Map my hospital's first improvement](/request-demo)

No expiry date or limited place is claimed. If there is no permissioned email segment or working destination, these remain drafts.

**By day 7, three useful signals:** (1) at least one valid, received request or verified direct founder reply from a relevant hospital; (2) at least one qualified booking with a named pathway and buyer role; (3) one held qualified demo or a dated, owned path to it. These are provisional gates, not statistical benchmarks. If none occurs, diagnose route receipt and qualified reach before declaring the concept wrong.

**Day-14 budget-increase metric:** fully loaded cost per **held qualified demo from a distinct new buying organization**, counting founder marketing time at an agreed internal hourly cost and any paid spend. If no qualified demo is held, the rate is **N/A** because its denominator is zero. Paid-only acquisition cost is **N/A** while no paid campaign runs. No increase is automatic. Consider a small paid test only after a working destination, approved budget, consent/measurement setup, a qualified-demo signal from organic distribution, and enough capacity to answer leads. The north star remains cumulative held qualified hospital demos toward the 90-day planning scenario of 12.

**Concept-wrong signal:** Several independent, relevant hospital operators can describe recent handoff problems but consistently say the cost is minor, the incumbent or narrow tool resolves them, and a first-pathway comparison is not worth an executive/committee conversation. One weak post or a broken form does not disprove the concept.
