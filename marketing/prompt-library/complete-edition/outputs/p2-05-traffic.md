**Priority stack.** (1) Warm introductions: named trust around a small buying universe. (2) Targeted founder outreach by email or LinkedIn: fast learning from a specific problem, not bulk lead volume. (3) CRM follow-up: every conversation has an owner and next step. (4) Founder LinkedIn posts: reusable public explanation and a path to the demo. (5) One search-friendly pathway/FAQ page at a time: a durable answer prospects can read before or after a call. The demo is the conversion experience all five feed into, with its own preparation time.

**Three zero-budget quick wins this week, each under two hours.** (1) In 45 minutes, identify five distinct hospital buying organizations and write one precise introduction request for a person who actually knows each buyer; log only relationships you truly have. (2) In 60 minutes, turn the synthetic “one patient, three handoffs” example into a short founder LinkedIn post and a private message variant pointing to a verified destination. (3) In 45 minutes, audit `/request-demo` with the site owner: submit a test request, confirm receipt and ownership, and record the gap before directing traffic there. If the deployed flow fails, use a verified direct scheduling/contact route only after it has been tested.

**Avoid right now.** Product Hunt and AppSumo reach the wrong procurement motion; broad paid video/social spend buys attention before a working conversion; a full newsletter and self-hosted community create recurring work without a validated audience; formal referral/review programs need real customer consent and proof. The library's channel statistics and posting-time suggestions are unverified context, not operating targets for Georgia.

## Part 3 — Traffic building system

**Phase 1, weeks 1–2 — first relevant visitors.** Gate zero is a reliable lead destination: confirm the deployed demo route, repair or choose a verified receipt path, and place a human owner in the CRM. In a five-hour week: 25 minutes daily checks, 30 minutes Monday review, 30 minutes monthly-review reserve, 60 minutes to build a 10-organization research list, 75 minutes for five tailored introduction/outreach attempts, 60 minutes for one founder post and its short message adaptation, and 20 minutes for replies and source capture (**300 minutes**). Ask each conversation how they found MediMind. The first “100 people” is a **stretch visibility test**, counted as unique public marketing-site visitors or identifiable relevant recipients, not proof of 100 qualified buyers; do not add email impressions, pageviews, and social reach into one number. **Readiness to phase 2:** at least one accepted demo request and one held qualified demo from a distinct organization, with source and next action logged; if zero after two weeks, inspect delivery first, then message/target fit. Engineering repairs and the sales meeting itself sit outside the marketing five hours.

**Phase 2, weeks 3–8 — repeat the signal.** Keep the top two sources of qualified conversations, publish one reusable pathway explainer per two weeks, and adapt it to one post, one direct outreach note, and one follow-up. Give every externally shared asset an ID and source tag. Add a small permission-based email follow-up only after opt-in and unsubscribe handling work; send no automated sequence to cold contacts by assumption. Each ordinary week: daily checks 25, weekly review 30, monthly-review reserve 30, account-specific outreach/introduction requests 75, content/adaptation 80, community/editor/partner contribution 30, and replies/CRM 30 minutes = **300**. **Readiness to phase 3:** a working accepted-submission path; at least four cumulative held qualified demos by week 8; a repeatable route from two sources to held meetings; an evaluation owner and readiness question in each serious account. Four is a planning gate, not a performance claim.

**Phase 3, month 3 onward — compound selectively.** By day 90, use the same five-hour ceiling to refresh the best public page, make one targeted partnership/editor contribution per month, and keep direct outreach and CRM follow-up. Ordinary-week allocation: daily checks 25, Monday review 30, monthly-review reserve 30, named-account outreach/follow-up 75, best-page or clip reuse 60, partner/editor contribution 40, and replies/CRM cleanup 40 minutes = **300**. Paid tests are optional **only after** there is an approved budget, consent/measurement review, a functioning form, enough qualified traffic to learn, and a credible landing page; their setup replaces the 60-minute creative slot. Protect the monthly deep-dive week by reducing creative output. A partner webinar or trade article replaces, rather than adds to, that week's content slot. **Readiness to scale:** the 12-demo scenario is met or there is a clear upward trend in unique held qualified demos from several buying organizations, three readiness-approved evaluations are plausible from named accounts, and delivery capacity exists. If paid cost per held qualified demo is unknown or too high against deal economics, stop the test. Month 6 planning remains conditional on measured conversion and real commercial capacity.
