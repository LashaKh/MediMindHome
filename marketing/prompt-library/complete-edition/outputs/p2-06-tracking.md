> **Read the metrics correctly.** The source library supplies benchmark percentages for broad B2B funnels. They are **unverified library comparators**, not MediMind's historical rates, Georgian hospital norms, or automatic targets. Below, absolute gates protect the launch; percentages become directional only after a meaningful denominator. A zero denominator is **N/A**, never 0% or red. Report both numerator and denominator and label any rate from fewer than 20 qualified organizations or fewer than 100 relevant page sessions **provisional**. Site traffic, LinkedIn impressions, booked calls, held meetings, trials and paying organizations are different events.

## Part 1 — Funnel tracking system

**Event and entity definitions.** A public-site visit is a unique GA4 user/session on the marketing site, after internal-traffic filtering. An accepted lead has a successful backend receipt and CRM record; a button click or client-side “thank you” state is not one. A qualified hospital organization is a deduplicated legal buying group with a relevant cross-department need, a decision/influence route, a candidate owner, and a plausible evaluation path. A *held qualified demo* has an actual meeting date, the defined qualification fields, and a unique organization ID; reschedules, repeats, anchor-account conversations and two attendees from the same group do not inflate the north star. An evaluation is readiness-approved only after scope, owners, access, baseline and stop criteria are agreed. Paid means a signed commercial agreement **and first revenue recognized/collected as the business defines it**, with contracted versus received amounts separately labelled. Store organization identity and contact details in an access-controlled CRM, never in GA4 parameters.

## Part 3 — UTM tracking system

A UTM tag is a short set of words added to a **link shared outside the site** so analytics can tell whether a visitor arrived from a founder post, email, partner or ad and which piece sent them. It does not identify a person or prove that channel caused the purchase. Keep identifiable names, hospital names, email addresses, clinical details and free-text answers out of URLs and analytics. Google explains how manually tagged campaign dimensions appear in GA4 [here](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11242870?hl=en-GB).

**Naming convention.** Lowercase ASCII and underscores only. `utm_source` = platform or sender class (`linkedin`, `email`, `partner`, `hospital_forum`, `direct_outreach`, `google`, `referral`); `utm_medium` = `social`, `email`, `partner`, `community`, `outreach`, `cpc`, or `referral`; `utm_campaign` = `one_patient_2026q4` for this campaign, never a person/account name; `utm_content` = catalog ID plus version (`t01_v1`, `m05_v1`) and optional approved variation (`_a`, `_b`). Source and medium describe *arrival*; content identifies the specific creative. Preserve both first-touch and latest external touch in CRM; if the buyer says a colleague forwarded it privately, record “self-reported peer forward” too. Do not overwrite a first source with an internal click.

**Destinations.** P01 `/pathway-assessment`; P02 `/hospital-os`; P03 `/case-study`; P04 `/webinar`; P05 `/request-demo`; P06 `/early-partner`; P07 `/compare`; P08 `/activation`; P09 `/referrals`; P10 `/evidence`; P11 `/sandbox`. These are **planned path names** except P05, the existing route. Even P05 must not be promoted until real backend acceptance, receipt and scheduling are checked. Do not share a planned URL until its page, form, analytics and privacy behavior work. For organic search landings and internal navigation, the URL is clean, without UTMs. For private customer documents, use a controlled link and CRM source note rather than a public tracking URL.

**Complete 85-piece channel-link library.** A row with a public external channel supplies a ready link structure; “private/none” is a deliberate no-UTM rule. The table is keyed to the exact catalog ID and its planned destination, with one line for every catalog piece. The “Native…” line shows the **clean destination URL** for search, direct access or internal navigation; it does not mean an externally shared LinkedIn, email, outreach, community or partner CTA should use that clean link. Use the matching tagged link on every controllable external CTA. Where a second public distribution channel is approved, use the same destination and ID with its own listed `source/medium`; this keeps the content identity while distinguishing the sender. Links are **planning URLs**, not a statement that the page exists.

## Part 4 — Weekly tracking routine

**Daily, five minutes (Monday–Friday; 25 minutes/week).** Look first at the CRM count of cumulative unique **held qualified demos** versus the 13-week pace, and at yesterday's accepted requests and owner assignment. A day with no demo is normal; a new unowned request or a form test that fails is not. If the pace is behind, inspect real accepted leads and next actions before looking at impressions. The CRM is the source of truth for the north star; GA4 is the source for site traffic. This five-minute block is included in Prompt 5's 300-minute total.

**Weekly, 30 minutes Monday (includes Prompt 7's 15-minute report).** In 5 minutes, record TOFU relevant reach, tagged site visits and accepted new leads; 5 minutes, MOFU lead-to-demo, permissioned email and any webinar/case asset (N/A if unlaunched); 5 minutes, BOFU held demos, evaluation decisions and sandbox first value (N/A if absent); 3 minutes, AFU activation/retention and support (N/A before customers); 2 minutes, LOFU referrals/reviews (N/A before eligible customers); 5 minutes, reconcile UTM first/latest source with self-reported source and check CRM owners/aging; 5 minutes, write the single biggest leak and next action. All rates show numerator/denominator. An absent product stage is N/A, not red.

**Monthly, two hours on first Monday, replacing ordinary creative work that week.** 20 minutes verify raw accepted submissions, distinct organizations and source completeness; 25 minutes compare asset ID → qualified conversations → held demos; 20 minutes compare the top two channels by *qualified* meetings, not clicks; 20 minutes inspect stage drop-offs, median lead-to-demo time and support/evaluation workload; 20 minutes reconcile month-over-month revenue and cohort eligibility; 15 minutes choose one asset to improve, one channel to reduce, and one experiment with a clear stop rule. Prompt 7's 30-minute monthly review is the summary produced inside this two-hour analysis, not additional founder time. In the first-month deep-dive week, the 120 minutes leaves 125 of the 300 weekly minutes for distribution after daily and weekly checks; no second 5-hour allocation is assumed.

## Part 6 — 90-day success dashboard

The following is **illustrative funnel math**, not a forecast: research 60 distinct target organizations over roughly 13 weeks (about 5/week); if 30 engage in a substantive exchange (50%), 18 accept a demo slot (60%), and 12 hold a qualified demo (67% of booked), the north star reaches 12. If 25% of those 12 approve an evaluation after readiness checks, that is 3; if one of those three becomes a *new* paying buying organization, that is 33% of evaluations. None of these rates is measured yet. Website signups can contribute, but outbound and introductions mean page-conversion benchmarks cannot be used alone to derive the target.

| Metric | Week 4 scenario | Week 8 scenario | Day 90 scenario | Current baseline | Gap to close |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Distinct researched hospital buying organizations | 20 | 40 | 60 | Unknown | Establish list and dedupe |
| Substantive two-way exchanges | 8 | 18 | 30 | Unknown | Test access/message |
| Accepted demo bookings, unique organizations | 3 | 7 | 18 | Unknown; current source handler is not proof of accepted leads | Repair capture, qualify |
| **Held qualified demos, unique organizations** | **2** | **4** | **12** | **Unknown** | Record held dates and organization IDs |
| Readiness-approved evaluations | 0–1 | 1 | 3 stretch | Unknown | Scope, owners, access, baseline |
| New paying buying organizations | 0 | 0 | 1 stretch | Founder reports pre-revenue; anchor agreement not counted as new paying | Agree scope and close independently |
| Public-site visitors, trailing month | 100 | 200 | 300 | Unknown | Tag and measure relevant sources |
| Accepted lead → CRM owner/task coverage | 100% after repair | 100% | 100% | Source handler not connected | Test every path |

**One number that matters most:** cumulative held qualified demos from distinct hospital buying organizations. Check against a staircase of 2 by week 4, 4 by week 8, and 12 by day 90, adjusting only as a labelled plan after evidence.

**Three early warnings in the first 30 days:** (1) a test request shows success without a stored lead and named owner; (2) researched, relevant outreach yields no substantive buyer reply across the first ten distinct organizations; (3) no held qualified demo by week 4 despite confirmed capture and conversations. Resolve instrumentation before declaring message failure.

**Three growth signals by day 90:** (1) 12 distinct held qualified demos, not repeated meetings; (2) at least three named evaluations pass readiness checks with a baseline and hospital owner; (3) one *new* paying organization plus delivery capacity, with acquisition source and commercial terms recorded. If only the first two occur, continue learning rather than claiming revenue traction.
