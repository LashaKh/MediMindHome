# Lasha's first-week launch guide

**Purpose.** Start one useful conversation with a Georgian private-hospital CEO or group operator about a repeated patient handoff, then offer a founder-led pathway demo. The campaign line is **“Why does one patient create so much extra work?”** The next action is **“Map my hospital's first improvement.”** The 90-day number—12 held qualified demos, three readiness-approved evaluations, and one new paying buying organization—is a planning scenario, not a forecast or a claim of existing traction. Count one buying organization once; keep the existing anchor separate.

## What is ready on paper

The [funnel blueprint](../outputs/p2-01-funnel.md) defines the stages and the full 85-idea [catalog](content-catalog.json). Those ideas are a backlog, **not 85 pieces to make**. The [angle plan](../outputs/p2-02-angles.md) chooses a low-risk map, one use case, and a controlled comparison. The [campaign script](../outputs/p2-03a-campaign-script.md) contains an 82-second founder phone video, a 15-second cut, and two short tests using synthetic information. The first public destination is P05, the existing `/request-demo` route. Its [proposed page copy](../outputs/p2-04-funnel-pages.md) says **“Map your first patient-pathway improvement”** and promises a 25-minute founder call; it is not live site copy.

The form is the gate. Source inspection found that its current submit handler waits, logs to the browser, and shows success without sending the request to a backend. That does not prove the deployed site behaves the same way, but **do not send campaign traffic to P05 until a live test confirms receipt, a named owner, and a truthful failure path**. The site/engineering owner handles implementation outside the five founder marketing hours. The existing page also says 30 minutes; align it with the proposed 25-minute demo before publication.

## T10: the worksheet to use this week

Copy these six prompts onto one page. The buyer can answer without disclosing patient identity or clinical details:

1. **Pathway:** Fill four boxes—request, result, next decision, and financial close—using a fictional example in public copy.
2. **Last incident:** What happened in a recent real handoff, described without names or patient details?
3. **People and systems:** Which teams touched it, and which tools or manual steps did each need?
4. **Repeat work:** What was re-entered, searched for, called about, or reconciled? Count steps and note who did them.
5. **First baseline:** What could be measured now—task time, repeat entries, wait time, exceptions, or appropriate actual collections? Record how the number is obtained; “unknown” is acceptable.
6. **Decision:** Who owns the work, who must review it clinically and technically, and what result would make a limited parallel evaluation worthwhile?

T10 is useful even if the buyer decides the incumbent can fix the problem. Do not convert a filled worksheet into a public case study or a performance claim without the hospital's review and permission.

## The first five hours, total

Use the [traffic chapter's](../outputs/p2-05-traffic.md) **300-minute weekly clock**, also used in the angle plan. Reserve **85 minutes** for five five-minute weekday checks (25 total), a 30-minute Monday scorecard review, and a 30-minute weekly share of the monthly deep dive. The remaining **215 minutes** are the entire creative and outreach allowance—not another five hours for each channel:

| Minutes | First-week action | Finished when |

| 40 | Answer replies, ask for the last concrete incident, and record the next owner/action. | A reply becomes a pathway discussion or a clear “not now,” not a vanity impression. |

The 3A video is **ready to film, not yet filmed**. Use the week-two creative slot in the angle plan for the phone shoot and captions, once the destination works. Do not add filming on top of week one's 300 minutes. A founder demo, clinical/IT workshop, technical route repair, and customer implementation each need separately planned time.

## Measure the real next step

The [tracking plan](../outputs/p2-06-tracking.md) distinguishes a click, an accepted request, a booked meeting, and a **held qualified demo**. The last is the north star. A qualified demo has a distinct hospital buying organization, relevant authority or sponsor, a recent cross-department problem, and an owner or path to one. Put source/content ID (T10, T01, or the later video), organization, owner, next date, and held-demo date in a controlled CRM or sheet. Keep names and clinical details out of public analytics URLs. If there is no eligible denominator, write **N/A** rather than a percentage.

At Monday's review, decide one thing: what most limits the next held qualified demo—lead receipt, access to the right buyer, message fit, or evaluation readiness? Fix that first. The page, worksheet, script, and reporting templates are drafted. No buyer interviews, campaign launch, external sends, new page deployment, accepted lead flow, customer outcome, or film production was performed by this guide.
