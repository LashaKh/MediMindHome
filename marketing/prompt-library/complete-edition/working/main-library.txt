PART 1
                   Describe Your Business & Market
PROMPT 1




THE GOAL OF THIS PROMPT

By the end of this prompt you will have a sharp, stress-tested one-sentence description of
your business, a clear picture of the real market you are operating in, the 3 most dangerous
assumptions your business is built on, a market sizing you can actually use and defend, a
map of the key players in your space, the gap your product fills, and a market positioning
statement ready for your pitch deck or investor conversation.




You are a startup strategist working with pre-seed founders. Your job is not to produce
polished reports — it is to help me make better decisions.




I am going to describe my business. Your job is to push back, sharpen, and stress-test
what I tell you — not just validate it.




My business in my own words:

[Paste 3-10 sentences. Do not polish it. Write it like you would explain it to a friend.]




Once I share this, do the following in order:




Step 1 — Restate it back to me sharply
Write one sentence that captures exactly what I do, who I do it for, and what changes for
them because of it. If you cannot write that sentence cleanly, tell me why — that gap is
my first problem to solve.




Step 2 — Name the real market I am in

Not the broad industry. The specific behavioral market — meaning: what are customers
currently doing to solve this problem without me? That is my real competition and my
real market.




Step 3 — Surface my 3 biggest assumptions

Based on what I have described, list the 3 assumptions my business is built on that, if
wrong, would kill the idea. Be direct. Do not soften them.




Step 4 — Market sizing I can actually use

Do not give me a top-down TAM slide number. Instead, tell me: how many specific,
findable, reachable people or businesses have this problem right now and are actively
looking for a solution? Walk me through the logic step by step so I can verify or
challenge it.




Step 5 — Identify the key players

List the incumbents, fast-growing startups, and adjacent players already competing for
the same customer attention or budget. For each one, note: what they do well, what they
do poorly, and why a customer might choose them over me today.




Step 6 — Pinpoint the gap
Based on everything above, where does the current market fall short? What is
underserved, overpriced, overcomplicated, or simply ignored? Be specific — not 'the
market lacks innovation' but exactly which customer, with exactly which unmet need.




Step 7 — Market positioning statement

Write one tight paragraph I can use in a pitch deck or investor conversation. It should
name who I serve, what I do, why it is different, and why now. Clear, confident, and
specific — no buzzwords.

PROMPT 2

product works from first touch to final outcome, the single moment of value where customers
first feel the product working, every friction point in the journey, a full JTBD analysis of what
customers are really hiring your product to do at each step, your market sophistication level
and what it means for your messaging, and a clean 150-word customer-facing product
description written in the language of the job to be done.

You are a product strategist and conversion copywriter working with pre-seed founders.
Your job is to extract the clearest possible description of how a product actually works —
not marketing language, not vision statements, but the real mechanical flow a customer
experiences from the moment they discover the product to the moment they get the
result.




My product in my own words:

[Describe what your product does. Do not worry about sounding polished. Even rough
notes work.]
Once I share this, do the following in order:




Step 1 — Map the customer journey in steps

Write out the exact sequence of steps a customer goes through — from first touch to
final outcome. Number each step. Be specific about what the customer does, what the
product does, and what happens as a result. If you do not have enough detail to
complete a step, flag it clearly — those gaps are product clarity problems I need to fix.




Step 2 — Identify the moment of value

Which single step is the moment the customer first feels the product is working for
them? This is my aha moment. Name it, describe it, and explain why it matters for
retention and marketing.




Step 3 — Identify friction points

Where in the journey is a customer most likely to drop off, get confused, or lose trust?
List each friction point and what is causing it — complexity, missing information, too
many steps, or unclear value.




Step 4 — Apply the JTBD lens

For each major step, tell me: what is the customer actually hiring this product to do at
that moment? Separate it into the three layers:
   •   Functional — what they want to accomplish
   •   Social — how they want to be perceived
   •   Emotional — how they want to feel
Step 5 — Identify my market sophistication level
Based on Eugene Schwartz's 5 levels — Hello Market, What Is It, How Does It Work, New
Mechanism, Experience — which level is my market currently at? What does that mean for how
I should be describing and marketing this product right now? Be specific about what messaging
approach fits my stage.

Step 6 — Rewrite the product description
Using everything from steps 1 to 5, write a clean, customer-facing description of how the
product works. It should be written in the language of the customer's job to be done — not
feature language. Maximum 150 words.

PROMPT 3

THE GOAL OF THIS PROMPT

By the end of this prompt you will have an exhaustive list of every feature your product has,
each feature translated into a real-world customer benefit, the emotional depth behind each
benefit using the JTBD framework, the top 5 customer guarantees or trust signals that reduce
buying risk, a complete BFG master table, and 3 versions of your strongest BFG combination
ready to test in marketing.


CLAUDE MEMORY COMMAND

Before you begin this prompt, say: "I have all previous answers stored and will use them

You are a conversion strategist working with pre-seed founders. Your job is to build the complete
BFG — Features, Benefits, and Guarantees — framework for my product so that every
marketing asset we create is grounded in specific, provable value.

Before proceeding, I need to answer these 7 questions:

   1. What are all the features of my product? List everything — even the ones that feel
        obvious or small.
   2.   For each feature, what does the customer actually get? What changes for them?
   3.   What is the single most important outcome a customer achieves by using this product?
   4.   What are the top 3 objections a potential customer has before buying?
   5.   What proof do I have that the product works — data, results, testimonials, case studies?
   6.   What guarantees can I honestly offer — money back, free trial, specific result, or other?
   7.   What do customers say when they describe why they chose this product?

[Answer all 7 questions before continuing.]

Once I share my answers, do the following in order:

Step 1 — List every feature
Take everything I have shared and produce a complete, organized list of every feature. Group
them by category if needed. Flag any feature that is unclear or needs more detail.

Step 2 — Convert every feature to a benefit
For each feature, write the direct customer benefit — what the customer gets, gains, or stops
suffering from because this feature exists. Write each benefit as a customer would say it, not as
a product spec.

Step 3 — Add the emotional benefit layer
For each benefit, identify the underlying emotional or social job it fulfills using the JTBD
framework. How does achieving this benefit make the customer feel? How does it change how
they are perceived?

Step 4 — Build the guarantees and trust layer
Based on my answers about proof and guarantees, build a list of the 5 strongest trust signals
and guarantees I can offer. For each one write: what it promises, why it reduces risk, and where
in the customer journey it should appear.

Step 5 — Build the BFG master table
Compile everything into a complete BFG master table with four columns: Feature, Benefit,
Emotional Job, and Guarantee or Trust Signal. This table becomes the source of truth for every
piece of marketing copy I write.

Step 6 — Identify my strongest BFG combination
Based on the full table, identify the single most compelling combination of feature, benefit,
emotional job, and guarantee. Write 3 versions of this combination as marketing statements —
one rational, one emotional, one identity-based. These are the starting points for my UVPs.

You are a positioning strategist working with pre-seed founders. Your job is to generate the
complete set of unique value propositions for my product and map them to the specific jobs my
customers are hiring it to do.


REQUIRED INPUTS
    8. Your business and market description from Prompt 1
    9. Your product mechanics from Prompt 2
    10. Your BFG master table from Prompt 3
    11. Answer: Who are the 2-3 distinct customer types who might buy this product? Describe
        each in 2-3 sentences.
    12. Answer: What is the single most important outcome each customer type is trying to

Once I share my inputs, do the following in order:

Step 1 — Map the full JTBD for each customer type
For each customer type, map their complete JTBD profile across three layers:
   • Functional job — the specific task or outcome they need to accomplish
   • Social job — how they want to be perceived by others as a result of solving this problem
   • Emotional job — how they want to feel after the problem is solved
For each JTBD also identify: the trigger moment — the specific event that makes the job
suddenly urgent, and what they were doing before — the previous solution or workaround they
were using.

Step 2 — Confirm market sophistication level
Based on everything we know about this market from Prompts 1 and 2, confirm or update the
market sophistication level. State clearly what level we are at and what this means for the

Step 3 — Generate 10 UVPs as brand promises
Write 10 UVPs for this product. Each UVP must be written as a direct promise to the customer
— not a feature description, not a mission statement, but the specific valuable change the
customer experiences. Calibrate every UVP to the market sophistication level confirmed in Step
2.

Step 4 — Score every UVP
Score each of the 10 UVPs across three criteria on a scale of 1 to 5: Clarity — how immediately
understandable is this promise, Credibility — how believable is this claim given what we can
prove, Desire — how strongly does this promise connect to the customer's emotional job.
Identify the top 3 UVPs by total score.

Step 5 — Match UVPs to customer types
For each of the top 3 UVPs identify which customer type it resonates with most strongly and
why. Note any UVP that works across multiple customer types.

Step 6 — Write the hero statement
Using the highest-scoring UVP and the strongest JTBD insight, write one hero statement — a
single sentence that captures the most powerful version of what this product does and why it
matters. This becomes the default headline for the homepage, the pitch deck opening, and the
first line of every ad.

Once I share my inputs, do the following in order:

Step 1 — Full competitive landscape
Map every relevant competitor across 4 categories:
  • Direct competitors — products that do what mine does for the same customer
  • Indirect competitors — different solutions to the same problem
   •   Status quo competitors — the spreadsheet, the workaround, the manual process my
       customer uses instead of buying anything
   •   Future competitors — well-funded players who do not compete today but could easily
       move into this space

Step 2 — Competitive comparison table
Build a comparison table across the 5 dimensions that matter most to my customer based on

Step 3 — Find the white space
Based on the comparison table, identify the specific combination of dimensions where no
competitor is strong and my product could win. This is my white space. Describe it in one
paragraph — be specific about which customer, which job, and which gap.

Step 4 — Write the defensible positioning statement
Using the white space identified in Step 3, write my positioning statement in this format: Unlike
[competitors], my product is the only [category] that [specific mechanism] so that [specific
customer outcome]. Then stress-test it — could any of my top 3 competitors claim this same
position honestly? If yes, sharpen it until the answer is no.

Explain why. Then give me 3 specific actions I can take now to strengthen my position against
them.

Write 5 talking points I can use when a potential customer mentions a competitor. Each talking
point should: acknowledge what the competitor does well, then pivot to the specific thing I do
differently that matters for their situation. These must be honest, specific, and conversational —
not corporate.
