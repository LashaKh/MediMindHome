## External primary sources

### E1. Hospital CEO priorities, U.S.

[American College of Healthcare Executives: Top Issues Confronting Hospitals, 2025 survey](https://www.ache.org/News-and-Insights/ACHE-Research/Research-Studies/Top-Issues-Confronting-Hospitals). Accessed 22 September 2026.

The October 2025 survey received 215 responses from 1,185 community-hospital CEOs (18%). Financial challenges ranked first, workforce second, and governmental mandates third. This supports investigating operational and financial buying reasons before assuming AI excitement leads demand. It does not establish rankings in Georgia or causation by software; U.S. payment-policy details should not be imported into Georgian copy.

### E2. Georgian health-system context

[European Observatory / WHO: Health systems in action 2024: Georgia](https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/publications/i/health-systems-in-action-georgia-2024), published 4 December 2024. Accessed 22 September 2026.

The country overview describes predominantly private-for-profit provision, complex coverage, and an acute nursing shortage. This makes private-provider economics and staff capacity relevant research topics. It is a country-level report, not a buyer-interview study, and does not establish software-related financial losses or current payment rules for a specific hospital. The linked page's metadata title is inconsistent, but its visible heading and content identify Georgia.

### E3. Hospital integration is possible, but uneven

[ONC/ASTP: Hospital Use of APIs to Enable Data Sharing between EHRs and Third-Party Technology](https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-use-of-apis-to-enable-data-sharing-between-ehrs-and-third-party-technology/), Data Brief 81, February 2026; 2024 AHA survey data. Accessed 22 September 2026.

The study describes widespread third-party data exchange, often through proprietary or other non-standardized methods, and variation by hospital characteristics. Its 2024 survey had 2,253 non-federal acute-care hospital respondents, a 51% response rate, with weighting. This is a reason to investigate integration quality, not to claim existing software cannot support AI or that total replacement is the only option. It is U.S. context, not local buyer evidence.

## Measurement rules

- Count realized collections, not merely charges identified. Separate reduced delay from new money recovered.
- Use legitimate, clinically accurate documentation and billing. Do not present inflated coding or unnecessary treatment as revenue opportunity.
- Staff minutes saved are capacity, not automatically cash savings. Identify whether overtime, staffing expense, throughput, or attention changes.
- Compare similar workflows/cases; account for training and process changes. Avoid counting the same benefit twice.
- Show total evaluation and transition costs. A matched subscription fee is not identical total cost of ownership.
- Process measures such as faster retrieval are not interchangeable with patient outcomes.
