# Hospital buyer research: reusable interview guide

Created: 22 September 2026
Status: Proposed plan and template. No outreach or interviews have been executed.

## Research question

Which recurring hospital problem is serious enough that a decision-maker will allocate money, staff time, and implementation effort to fix it, and what proof makes switching acceptable?

Use the first part of the conversation to learn about actual behavior, not to pitch MediMind or ask whether AI sounds valuable.

## Initial sample

Aim for 8-10 conversations over two weeks, covering at least four independent hospital organizations where access allows. This is a practical discovery starting point, not statistical representation or guaranteed saturation.

- Economic buyers: 4-5 CEOs, owners, or finance leaders. Include organizations beyond the anchor relationship.
- Clinical/operations leaders: 2-3 people who own daily work and can show a concrete example.
- IT/implementation leaders: 2 people, including someone involved in a postponed or unsuccessful switch if accessible.

Seek three buying situations across the sample: actively evaluating change, living with the problem but not evaluating, and recently changed or postponed replacement. Do not count several people at one hospital as independent confirmations of market prevalence.

## A 25-minute conversation

### Opening: 2 minutes

Explain that we are studying where hospital operations and software create unnecessary work and cost. There are no right answers; we want examples of what happened, including situations where the existing system works well. Ask permission to take notes or record. Do not request identifiable patient information.

### Context: 3 minutes

1. What two results are you personally responsible for improving this quarter?
2. How are you measured, and who reviews those results?

### Recent events and consequences: 12 minutes

1. Tell me about the most recent time your systems made one of those results harder to achieve. Walk me through what happened.
2. Who had to do extra work? What did they do, and how often does this happen?
3. What was the consequence: money, time, delay, staff frustration, or risk? Is there a record we can review in de-identified or aggregated form?
4. What have you already tried? What did it cost, and what worked or did not work?
5. Why has the problem not been fixed? Who or what limits the options?
6. What event would make it necessary to act this quarter rather than later?
7. Tell me about the last major software purchase or replacement decision. Who approved it, who could stop it, and what evidence mattered?

Do not prompt with our pain list until they have described their own priorities. Ask for counterexamples and what their current supplier does well.

### Evaluation and close: 8 minutes

1. What would a new system have to demonstrate before you considered replacing anything?
2. What would make an evaluation too disruptive or risky to attempt?
3. What would success after a limited evaluation look like, and who would verify it?
4. Is AI attached to an actual project, owner, budget, or deadline today? If yes, what is that project meant to change?
5. Who else sees this problem differently and should be involved?

Only after discovery, show a short message concept and ask: What do you think this offers? Which part matters? Which part is hard to believe? What is missing? A polite compliment is not evidence of willingness to buy.

## Role-specific probes

### CEO / owner / finance

- Which loss or cost can you quantify, and which do you only suspect?
- For a payment problem, is the cause documentation, submission, payer policy, rates, or something else?
- What decision is held up because you cannot get reliable information?
- What would justify the hospital's internal time spent changing systems?
- Who owns budget approval, and what happens if nothing changes?

### Clinical / operations leader

- Show the last handoff or task that required repeated checking or entry, without patient identifiers.
- Where was relevant information unavailable when needed?
- Which tasks should be simplified, and which checks must remain?
- What did the last software change make better or worse?
- What would you refuse to change without a safety review?

### IT / implementation leader

- Which existing connections work well, and which repeatedly fail?
- What access, data quality, external interfaces, and approvals does a parallel evaluation require?
- What extra work would your team have to do?
- What are the non-negotiable requirements for reliability, security, support, data export, and exit?
- What caused the last migration to stall, and what proof would have changed that decision?

## Decision rule for the next copy round

Prefer the pain supported by recurring recent incidents across independent target hospitals, meaningful consequences, failed or costly workarounds, and an actionable decision path. Three independent organizations is a useful early screening signal, not statistical validation.

Choose one outcome to demonstrate in a scoped evaluation. Agree on its baseline, measurement method, timing, acceptance threshold, staff burden, safety constraints, and stop conditions. Avoid invented percentage targets before seeing the baseline.

Then test three different messages: financial/operational control, staff time and care coordination, and modernization with manageable transition risk. Compare understanding, belief, fit, and concrete next steps rather than asking which slogan sounds nicest.
