## The big picture first

The script already does the hard parts well. It has a real story (the 2 a.m. case), a
solution that answers that exact story, strong traction, and a clear vision. Most fixes
are about **picking fewer facts and saying them more simply**, which is the core of
Dina's talk.

Three things to know before reading the sections:

1. **Length.** Dina said a 3-minute pitch fits about **300 words** (1:28:38). This script is
   about **550 words of speech**. The rewrites below keep your length close, as asked. If demo
   day is 3 minutes, you will need to cut. A cut plan is at the end.
2. **Dina already reviewed our traction line live** (1:09:59 to 1:12:35, with Irakli). Her
   advice is used in the Validation rewrite.
3. **Numbers marked [confirm]** came from the live session (automatic transcript) or need
   checking. Do not say them on stage until Irakli confirms them.

Must-keep lines are kept word for word: "making us a category-defining company", the
agentic AI harness, the 2 a.m. pancreatitis case, and "One patient walks in. The whole
hospital moves."

## 1. Cover

> In four years, practicing medicine without an AI will be malpractice. Hello, I'm Lasha, a cardiologist, and I'm building that future with my team today.

**Already follows**
- Opens with a bold line people will remember. Dina: make them remember one key thing (7:35).
- Credibility up front: a practising cardiologist. This is Dina's "personal credibility" opening style (1:26:54).

**Breaks**
- Nothing major. "Building that future with my team today" is a little soft at the end of a strong opener.

**Suggested rewrite (same length)**
> In four years, practicing medicine without an AI will be malpractice. Hello, I'm Lasha. I'm a cardiologist, and with my team, we're building that future today.

## 2. Validation

> In 15 months, we went from an idea to a signed contract with Georgia's second-largest hospital chain, which has 25 hospitals. Last week, we reached an agreement with a second huge hospital to replace its full software stack. That makes us responsible for a platform that handles around 2 million patient encounters. They will soon become one of the first fully AI-native hospital networks in the world.

**Already follows**
- Leads with traction, one of Dina's four good ways to open (1:24:57).
- Speed story ("15 months from idea to contract") creates FOMO (fear of missing out) (7:35, 57:56).
- Uses "one of the first", not "the first".

**Breaks**
- **"Georgia's second-largest"**: Dina said live, "it's a little too much detail" (1:10:36). Investors abroad do not know the ranking. Size does the work.
- **"A second huge hospital"**: Dina's exact point. Going from a big network to "a second one" sounds smaller. Frame it by its size (1:12:05, 1:12:35).
- **Too many numbers close together** (15 months, 25 hospitals, 2 million). They "get mushed together" (1:04:07). Dina said the most exciting number is the patient number (1:10:36).
- "Full software stack" is tech talk. Say "all its software".

**Suggested rewrite**
> In 15 months, we went from an idea to a signed contract with a network of 25 hospitals and about 1,000 doctors [confirm]. They are moving 1.1 million patient records onto MediMind [confirm]. Last week, another large hospital, with about 600,000 patients a year [confirm], agreed to replace all its software with ours. Soon, they will be one of the first fully AI-native hospital networks in the world.

*Why:* this is close to the line Dina wrote for you live. Check: is it 1.1 million records
"already moved" or "being moved"? Is 600,000 patients or encounters per year? Irakli
raised this himself (1:12:28). Dina: frame it well, but never in a way an investor finds
untrue (1:12:35). If the confirmed number is "2 million encounters a year" for both, use
that once instead of two patient numbers.

## 3. Growth / All-in

> Thirteen months ago, I thought an AI copilot would fix healthcare. I was wrong. The problem isn't a missing feature. It's the whole legacy system. We're doctors building for doctors. With AI engineering and a lean team, we're building a proactive OS that replaces every other piece of software a hospital needs, all in one codebase.

**Already follows**
- An honest "I was wrong" moment. It is a realisation story, like Dina's Neolink example (1:26:54).
- "Doctors building for doctors" is short and memorable.

**Breaks**
- **"Thirteen months" right after "15 months"** sounds like a contradiction. Two time numbers in a row blur.
- **Jargon:** "proactive OS", "AI engineering", "one codebase", "legacy system". Dina: explain it like I'm five; no industry jargon (14:58, 39:24).
- Leans on AI words. Dina: "everyone's an AI something"; say what it does instead (41:05).
- Positioning rule: say "operating system", not "OS".

**Suggested rewrite**
> A year ago, I thought an AI copilot would fix healthcare. I was wrong. The problem isn't a missing feature. It's the whole old system underneath. We're doctors building for doctors. With a lean team that uses AI to build fast, we're building a proactive hospital operating system. It replaces every other piece of software a hospital needs. One system, instead of many.

*Note:* keep "Thirteen months" if the exact date matters to you, but then cut "15 months"
from Validation, or the audience will try to do maths.

## 4. Case → Problem

> In one case from my practice, a patient came into the ER at 2 a.m. with stomach pain after a heart procedure. We were so focused on his heart that we missed the real culprit, his pancreas. The subtle signs were hidden in his long, complicated medical history. Because of that, he spent the next 20 days in the ICU and narrowly escaped death.
>
> In medicine, the right tools in the right systems should not be optional. They are critically important.
>
> At 2 a.m., people miss things. MediMind doesn't.

**Already follows**
- Told from your own experience, with specific detail (2 a.m., after a heart procedure, 20 days in ICU). This is exactly what Dina pushed the Moonlight team to add (27:14).
- Has the **why**: the signs were buried in a long history (36:16, "make sure you have that secondary").
- "At 2 a.m., people miss things. MediMind doesn't." is a strong, short line.

**Breaks**
- **No number.** Dina: quantify the problem, "bonus, but really just do it" (15:04). One case shows it can happen. A number shows it happens a lot, and costs a lot.
- **"The right tools in the right systems should not be optional..."** is abstract. It is a "toxic chemicals bad" sentence: true, but no new information (15:04). Replace it with the number.

**Suggested rewrite**
> In one case from my practice, a patient came into the ER at 2 a.m. with stomach pain after a heart procedure. We were so focused on his heart that we missed the real culprit, his pancreas. The subtle signs were hidden in his long, complicated medical history. Because of that, he spent the next 20 days in the ICU and narrowly escaped death.
>
> This is not rare. [One verified number: how often key signs are missed in busy hospitals, or what one avoidable ICU stay costs.]
>
> At 2 a.m., people miss things. MediMind doesn't.

*Tip:* pick a number about people or money, not a long-term study. Dina told the
Moonlight team a 10-year statistic was "too nerdy" (31:06).

## 5. Team

> My co-founder, Irakli, has a decade of experience in tech, and we're backed by a brilliant advisory board.

**Already follows**
- Short. Dina said team is "more straightforward, just delivering facts" (1:15:52).
- Industry box (a cardiologist) is already shown on the cover.

**Breaks**
- **Vague.** "A decade in tech" and "brilliant" are claims with no proof. Dina's good examples name a real thing: "My CTO developed a telemetry service that tracks 30,000 cleaning vehicles" (1:21:21).
- **Three boxes:** industry, technical, go-to-market (1:19:34). Go-to-market (selling) is not named. Your traction partly covers it (Dina: less needed with good traction), but say who closed those hospital deals.
- Does not answer Dina's key team question: if someone copies you, why do you still win? (1:19:34)

**Suggested rewrite**
> My co-founder, Irakli, has spent ten years building [one concrete thing, with a size]. I still practise in the hospitals we serve, and together we closed both hospital deals ourselves. Our advisers include [one named credential].

## 6. Competition

> Our competitors are AI tools, AI overlays, and legacy EMRs. None of them runs a hospital proactively. MediMind does.
>
> We replace all three with one hospital system and an agentic AI harness that does all the work staff do by hand today, making us a category-defining company.

**Already follows**
- Shows how you differ without a long rival list, like Dina's meditation-app example (42:47).
- "None of them runs a hospital proactively. MediMind does." is clear and short.

**Breaks**
- **Jargon:** "AI overlays", "legacy EMRs", "agentic AI harness". Most investors will not know them (39:24). The harness must stay, so explain it in a few words.
- **"Does all the work staff do by hand today"** is a very big claim. Dina: claims need proof (51:54). It also goes beyond what the first offer includes. Say what it does now, or call it the goal.
- **Missing the "why can't OpenAI just build this?" answer** (39:24). Your best answer (live hospitals, real data, your hospital as a lab) sits later, in Georgia. Point to it here in a few words.

**Suggested rewrite**
> Our competitors are AI tools, AI add-ons, and old medical record systems. Each fixes one piece. None of them runs a hospital proactively. MediMind does.
>
> We replace all three with one hospital system and an agentic AI harness, the layer that lets AI take on the routine work staff do by hand today, making us a category-defining company.

## 7. What we are

> We're building an AI that can run the whole hospital and make medical decisions within pre-authorized limits set by hospitals.
>
> Picture that same 2 a.m. case with MediMind running. It spots the signs of pancreatitis and decides what to do next. It orders the right blood tests, calls in the specialists, arranges the scans, books an ICU bed, and handles the billing.
>
> One patient walks in. The whole hospital moves.

**Already follows**
- **This is the best part of the script.** The solution answers the exact problem story. Dina: the solution must match the problem (37:33).
- Features are shown as actions in a story, not a feature list.
- "One patient walks in. The whole hospital moves." is the line people will remember.
- "Within pre-authorized limits set by hospitals" answers the safety worry before it is asked.

**Breaks**
- **It comes too late.** The case is in section 4, then team and competition interrupt, then the answer arrives. Dina's classic flow is problem → build up → product reveal (1:21:21). Move this section straight after the case.
- **No benefit number.** Dina: features plus quantified benefits (37:33). For example, time from arrival to the right diagnosis, once measured.
- "Make medical decisions" is strong. Keep it tied to the limits, and say it is what you are building, as the script already does.

**Suggested rewrite (light touch)**
> We're building a hospital operating system with an AI that can run the whole hospital and make medical decisions within limits each hospital sets in advance.
>
> Picture that same 2 a.m. case with MediMind running. It spots the signs of pancreatitis and decides what to do next. It orders the right blood tests, calls in the specialists, arranges the scans, books an ICU bed, and handles the billing. [Optional, once measured: the time saved.]
>
> One patient walks in. The whole hospital moves.

## 8. Georgia + rare combination

> Our choice of Georgia was no coincidence. It's a world hub for first-in-human trials, so what we prove here counts in the West. And we can prove it much sooner than we could in the West. This scale of regulatory freedom is simply not possible there.
>
> Our relaxed rules let us run an agentic clinical AI harness in live hospitals today. We build and validate in a week what takes the West years to clear. My hospital is our lab, and we have free access to any department or insight.

**Already follows**
- This is your **defensibility** (what is hard to copy). Dina: unique access to a partnership or data is what made 500 say yes, and it answers "why can't others just build this?" (7:35, 39:24).
- "My hospital is our lab" is short and memorable.

**Breaks**
- **"Relaxed rules" and "regulatory freedom"** can sound like cutting corners to a US investor. Frame it as speed with oversight, not fewer rules.
- **"The West" four times** in six sentences. Repetition costs words.
- **"A week vs years"** is a claim. Keep it only if you can back it when asked (51:54).
- "World hub for first-in-human trials" needs a source ready (Dina: no sources on slides, but have them on hand) (1:15:52).

**Suggested rewrite**
> We chose Georgia on purpose. It's a world hub for first-in-human trials, so what we prove here counts in the West, and we can prove it years sooner.
>
> Here, we can run an agentic clinical AI harness in live hospitals today, under limits each hospital sets. We build and test in a week what takes years to approve in the West. My hospital is our lab, with open access to every department.

## 9. Ambition

> Georgia is our validation and our free ticket into a huge value-based market, where we'll arrive already proven. We've almost finished our first full integration with our anchor chain of 25 hospitals, which opens almost any door in the local market and across the region.
>
> We strongly believe that within nine months, we'll dominate the Georgian market and have a strong foothold in Central Asia.
>
> By the third quarter of next year, we plan to raise 5 million to approach the U.S. market with the validation we've collected here.

**Already follows**
- Clear plan: Georgia first, then the region, then the US, with a funding amount and date.
- Shows focus on where you start (1:17:09).

**Breaks**
- **No market size.** Dina: say the size, what the market is, where, and where you start (1:19:09). "A huge value-based market" says none of these, and "value-based" is jargon.
- **Traction hiding here.** "Almost finished our first full integration" is traction. It belongs in Validation, or cut it.
- **No vision ending.** Dina: "wrap with a beautiful vision of the future" (1:28:38). The script ends on a fundraising date, which is the weakest last line in the pitch.
- "Dominate" in nine months is a big claim. Keep it if you can defend it.

**Suggested rewrite**
> Georgia is our proof, and our ticket to the US, where we'll arrive already proven. Next is a $379 million a year market across Georgia and nine nearby countries [confirm against the deck], starting with large hospital networks.
>
> Within nine months, we plan to lead the Georgian market and have a strong foothold in Central Asia. By the third quarter of next year, we plan to raise 5 million to enter the U.S. with the proof we've built here.
>
> [One-line vision to close, for example: a future where no patient is missed at 2 a.m., in any hospital, anywhere.]

## Suggested order

Dina's classic flow is problem → solution reveal → market, team, vision (1:21:21). Leading
with traction is also allowed (1:24:57), so keep your opening. Only one move is needed:

**Cover → Validation → Growth → Case → What we are → Competition → Georgia → Team → Ambition (with vision)**

This puts the answer ("One patient walks in...") right after the 2 a.m. case, while the
story is still fresh.

## If demo day is 3 minutes: a cut plan to reach about 300 words

Cut in this order until you fit:
1. The "right tools in the right systems" lines (Case).
2. The integration sentence in Ambition (already in Validation).
3. Georgia down to two sentences: trials hub + "my hospital is our lab".
4. Growth down to: "I thought an AI copilot would fix healthcare. I was wrong. The whole system was the problem."
5. Competition down to: the two "MediMind does" lines + the harness sentence.

Then rehearse until you **know it by heart**, not memorise it. Dina: memorising shows
in your eyes; knowing it lets you use your hands and read the room (9:19).
