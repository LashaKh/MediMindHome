Two examples the owner feels directly, chosen from many, each tied to a problem from slide 5. Billing answers the first: an invoice that is built from a complete record, reconciled to the bank and corrected with a trace is the invoice that does not come back as a returned claim or a fine. The warehouse answers the second: inventory is one of the largest balances a hospital holds, and it is where auditors most often find stock records they cannot verify or write-downs nobody saw coming; a matched, itemized warehouse is what makes those numbers reliable. Billing: almost nobody in Georgia has billing infrastructure that gives good reports, lets a correction be recorded simply, and connects to the bank so receipts match invoices automatically. Analytics are bad because the input is bad; fix the input and the reports become usable, including indirect cost by a rule. Warehouse: the pain is matching. RS receipts coming in automatically against a clean nomenclature, with batches and expiry tracked, removes a whole category of manual work.
