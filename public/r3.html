05  ·  Evidence

Evidence accumulates from go-live, in four layers.

We do not start collecting evidence in 2027. It accumulates from deployment. 2027 is when the dataset is mature enough for formal retrospective studies, and 2028 is the publication milestone.

Layer
What it gives us
What it does not
Layer 1
Historical legacy baseline
The hospital's pre-MediMind data, which we hold because we migrated it: throughput, turnaround times, utilisation, reimbursement outcomes, documentation completeness and length of stay.
Legacy data is fragmented, incomplete and sometimes poorly structured. Context, not clean ground truth.
Layer 2 · Phase 1
Clean prospective baseline
One system of record observes orders, labs, documentation, billing, timestamps, resource usage, clinician actions and outcomes. An internally consistent baseline the legacy stack could never produce.
Quality depends on completing the rollout. Billing and ministry reporting must be live for the economic lines.
Layer 3 · Phase 2
AI workflow impact
The same processes measured against both baselines as departmental AI goes live, controlling for volume and case mix. The fastest evidence: weeks on high-volume workflows.
Operational attribution, not causal proof. We say "plausibly attributable to MediMind," never "caused by."
Layer 4 · Phase 3
Decision support in shadow mode
What MediMind would have detected or recommended, compared against actual care, eventual outcomes and clinician review, per intervention class, before that class is enabled.
Horizons differ: process and safety signals in weeks, rarer clinical events in months, publications longer.
Where the economic buyer already loses money: physician overspend on DRG treatment paths, documentation that fails government reimbursement at full value, and ICU days caused by delayed diagnosis, which the hospital pays for.
What we do not claim yet: a dollar-saving number. Operational attribution is measurable relatively quickly. Clinical causality requires a higher evidence bar, and that is what the shadow-mode and publication track is for.

06  ·  Model validation

Not a number yet. A framework, per intervention.

We do not have clinically valid false-positive and false-negative rates, because Phase 3 is not live. The framework is explicit: intervention-specific, risk-weighted, and launched in shadow mode so we measure sensitivity and false-alert burden per workflow before any clinician depends on it.

Escalation ladder · the acceptable error changes at every rung
01
Detect and observeShadow mode. Record what would have been flagged, and score it against actual outcomes and clinician review.
02
Surface a flagAdvisory only. The design bias is toward sensitivity for catastrophic misses, and the false-positive cost stays low because nothing acts.
03
Recommend an actionSourced from established guidelines and rule books. The AI connects the patient context and detects that the rule has become relevant.
04
Require clinician confirmationFor disruptive, expensive or clinically consequential actions, the evidence threshold and the human-control requirement both go up.
05
Automate, narrowlyOnly narrowly defined, validated actions, and only eventually. The doctor remains the decision maker in every pipeline.
  • The unit of measurement is the intervention, not the model. "Which rate?" is answered with "for which action, in which risk class."
  • The threshold depends on the cost of being wrong. Before a workflow is enabled we classify the consequence, define the threshold and the human-control requirement, and validate both with clinical leadership.
  • Ground truth depends on the workflow. Objective events where they exist; otherwise the subsequent record plus retrospective clinician adjudication. The original clinician action is not treated as truth.
Metrics we will report
  • Sensitivity · false-negative rate
  • PPV · false-positive rate
  • False alerts per clinician shift
  • Time-to-detection vs existing workflow
Framework status

Draft, based on international clinical-AI practice, pending validation with clinical leadership before activation.

07  ·  The buying committee

Three people in the room. One is losing money.

The one hospital network we sold was bought by its owner and CEO, against money he was already losing, not against the software line, which is small in this market. The clinical case and the commercial case are sold together, to different people in the same room.

Economic buyer
Hospital owner · CEO · CFO

Buys money already being lost, not the software line item. Unified data and stopped leakage, both visible only once the data is unified. In this market the owner is usually in the room and understands DRG overspend directly.

Clinical champion
Department heads · lab lead · nursing lead

Hours returned. One login, one chart. Unlimited feature requests, turned into testable product in hours, which no incumbent offers. Our CTO sells here from inside the profession.

Blocker
IT function · incumbent vendor

Neutralised structurally by the parallel run: nothing is switched off until the hospital chooses.

Where the economic buyer already loses money
DRG overspend

Physicians overspending on treatment paths, with extras the reimbursement never covers.

Reimbursement shortfall

Unstructured, low-quality documentation means the government does not reimburse at full value.

ICU days

In the case from our own practice, a missed diagnosis cost twenty ICU days. The hospital pays for those days.

● full  ·  ◐ partial  ·  ○ no  ·  – not applicable.   Our own two partials are deliberate: the proactive layer is in testing rather than live, and only one pricing tier is contracted. Medplum is our data layer, not a competitor; it published the case study on our deployment.

Why all six conditions matter

Owning the system of record is what lets one intelligence observe a lab event, reason over the record, coordinate the next workflow and close the billing loop, without stitching five vendors together.

Where the incumbents lead us
  • Installed base. Thousands of hospitals against our one, with 25 contracted.
  • Regulatory clearance. FDA and CE marking we will not hold before 2029.
  • Published evidence. Decades of outcome studies; our first are targeted for 2028.
  • Specialty depth. Forty years of clinical content we have not built.
The one we watch most carefully

Oracle's next-generation EHR, described as built ground-up with the latest AI. "AI-native" alone no longer differentiates. What survives the comparison: FHIR R4 as the storage primitive rather than an API layer, whole-hospital coordination as the design goal, and demonstrated deployment speed.
