05 · Cost and time savings for the hospital owner?

Evidence accumulates from go-live, in four layers.

LAYER 1Historical legacy baseline
The hospital's pre-MediMind data, which we hold because we migrated it: throughput, turnaround, utilisation, reimbursement outcomes, documentation completeness, length of stay.
Fragmented, incomplete, sometimes poorly structured. Context, not clean ground truth.
LAYER 2 · PHASE 1Clean prospective baseline
One system of record observes orders, labs, documentation, billing, timestamps, resource use, clinician actions and outcomes. A consistent baseline the legacy stack could never produce.
Quality depends on completing the rollout; billing and ministry reporting must be live for the economic lines.
LAYER 3 · PHASE 2AI workflow impact
The same processes measured against both baselines as departmental AI goes live, controlling for volume and case mix. The fastest evidence: weeks on high-volume workflows.
Operational attribution, not causal proof. "Plausibly attributable to MediMind," never "caused by."
LAYER 4 · PHASE 3Clinical decision support in shadow mode
What MediMind would have detected or recommended, compared against actual care, outcomes and clinician review, per intervention class, before that class is enabled.
Horizons differ: process and safety signals in weeks, rarer clinical events in months, publications longer.
Where the owner already loses money: physician overspend on DRG treatment paths · documentation failing government reimbursement at full value · ICU days from delayed diagnosis, paid by the hospital.
What we do not claim yet: a dollar-saving number. Operational attribution is measurable quickly; clinical causality needs a higher bar and gets it: the Phase 3 and publication track.
SAY THISWe don't start collecting evidence in 2027. Evidence accumulates from deployment. 2027 is when the dataset is mature enough for formal retrospective studies; 2028 is the publication milestone.
06 · What are your false-positive and false-negative rates?

Not a number yet. A framework, per intervention.

ESCALATION LADDER · ACCEPTABLE ERROR CHANGES AT EVERY RUNGhuman control ↑ · evidence bar ↑
1
Detect and observeShadow mode. Record what would have been flagged; score against outcomes and clinician review.
2
Surface a flagAdvisory only. Design bias toward sensitivity for catastrophic misses; false-positive cost stays low because nothing acts.
3
Recommend an action"Consider pancreatitis, order lipase." Sourced from established guidelines and rules; AI connects the patient context.
4
Require clinician confirmationDisruptive, expensive or clinically consequential actions: higher evidence threshold, explicit human control.
5
Automate, narrowlyOnly validated, narrowly defined actions, eventually. The doctor stays the decision maker in every pipeline.
  • The unit of measurement is the intervention, not the model. "Which rate?" is answered with "for which action, in which risk class."
  • The threshold depends on the cost of being wrong. Consequence classified, threshold and human-control requirement defined, validated with clinical leadership before activation.
  • Ground truth depends on the workflow. Objective events where they exist (lab result, interaction, rejection, confirmed diagnosis); otherwise the subsequent record plus retrospective clinician adjudication. The original clinician action is not treated as truth.
  • Shadow mode creates the evidence before dependence. From network go-live, per class of intervention, before that class is enabled.
Metrics we will report · no targets stated
  • Sensitivity · false-negative rate
  • PPV · false-positive rate
  • False alerts per patient / per clinician shift
  • Time-to-detection vs existing workflow
framework status Draft, based on international clinical-AI practice. Pending validation with clinical leadership. Can be sent.
SAY THISWe don't have clinically valid rates yet because Phase 3 isn't live, but the validation framework is explicit: intervention-specific, risk-weighted, launched in shadow mode so we measure sensitivity and false-alert burden per workflow before anyone depends on it.

The one we watch most carefully

Oracle's next-generation EHR, described as built ground-up with AI. "AI-native" alone no longer differentiates. What survives that comparison: FHIR R4 as the storage primitive, not an API layer · proactive whole-hospital coordination as the design goal · full hospital-OS breadth · demonstrated deployment speed · our market focus.

Why all six, not "a bad idea nobody tries"

Owning the system of record is what lets one intelligence observe a lab event, reason over the longitudinal record, coordinate the next workflow, and close the billing loop, without stitching five vendors together.
